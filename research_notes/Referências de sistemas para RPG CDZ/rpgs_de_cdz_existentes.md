# Existing Saint Seiya / Cavaleiros do Zodíaco / 聖闘士星矢 tabletop RPGs: catalogue, mechanics, reception

> **How these notes were made (read first).** Every web page fetch (WebFetch, and curl through the proxy) was refused by the organization's egress policy. That covered scribd, archive.org, itch.io, legrog, sden, movimentorpg, wordpress, wikipedia and bitbucket. Reddit was also closed to the search tool. The session's shared web-search budget ran out after about 35 queries, before the Japanese, "official products" and 3DeT Victory/Biblioteca Élfica detail queries could run. **So every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries and snippets, not from reading the rulebooks in full.** Numbers and rule names are reported as the snippets gave them and should be checked against the PDFs before anyone relies on them. The label "(snippet)" is left implicit throughout. Where I could not tell which page in a result set a claim came from, I say so.

## Q1. Which Saint Seiya RPGs exist (official vs fan-made, by country/language, with dates/versions)?

### Takeaway
I found **no officially licensed (Toei/Kurumada/Shueisha) Saint Seiya tabletop RPG** in any language, and nothing at all for Japan. The closest things to "official" are Brazilian. The RPG magazine *Dragão Brasil* printed Cavaleiros do Zodíaco (CDZ) rules material in at least three issues, and Jambô, the current 3DeT publisher, posted a CDZ adaptation for 3DeT Victory on its official blog. Neither is confirmed as licensed. Everything else is fan-made. The fan scene is large and clusters in three places:
- **Brazil:** 3D&T/3D&T Alpha adaptations, a 12+ netbook Daemon line, plus GURPS and Fate Acelerado conversions.
- **France:** the long-running ANS and Saint Seiya Legend, plus the 2020 itch.io jam *Brûle ton Cosmos*, which produced about 11 games. The most polished of these is Romain d'Huissier's *Les Paladins du Zodiaque*.
- **Spain and the English-language forums:** Guardianes de Atenea, a Chasis-based beta, a Mutants & Masterminds (M&M) conversion blog, and a Weapons of the Gods conversion.

## Q2. How do these games handle Cosmo, the Senses, speed, cloths, techniques, ranks, non-Athena factions, "same technique doesn't work twice", and getting back up?

### Takeaway
The common pattern is:
- **Cosmo** is a *spendable pool* that turns into dice, automatic successes or technique power. A separate *Cosmo level/rating* marks power tier.
- **The Seventh Sense** is almost always a *binary switch* (an advantage, a free invoke, or "all tests easy").
- **Cloths** are a *bundle of existing advantages plus one unique ability per constellation*.
- **Techniques** are either buy-your-own-modifiers or a skill plus a named technique.
- **Getting back up** comes from willpower spends, negative-HP rules, or "heroes only die if deliberately finished off."

I found **no system that codifies "the same technique doesn't work twice on a Saint."**

### Cited Findings

**Cosmo: resource, stat or escalation?**
- **ANS (d10 pool):**
  - "1 point of cosmos = 2 automatic raw successes," and cosmos is what lets a character use secret techniques.
  - Willpower is a separate pool: 1 point = 2 extra dice to push past limits.
  - Sources: [ANS rules v3.1](http://www.thyamath.fr/Les_chevaliers_du_zodiaque/ANS-Le_Livre_des_regles_v3.1.pdf); [dokumen.tips mirror](https://dokumen.tips/documents/jeu-de-rle-saint-ans-est-un-jeu-de-rle-fait-par-les-fans-et-pour-les-fans-il.html)
- **Saint Seiya Legend:**
  - Each character has a cosmos level from 100 (ordinary humans) to 900+ (gods).
  - Characters "can add part of their cosmos to certain rolls to boost them."
  - Resolution is attribute + skill + 1d10 against a difficulty.
  - Sources: [SDEN](https://www.sden.org/saint-seiya-legend/Saint-Seiya-Legend,4265); [jdrp.fr](http://www.jdrp.fr/jdr/saint-seiya-legend-123.html)
  - Conflict: one snippet says 6 attributes (3 physical, 3 mental) plus cosmos level; another says derived stats (HP, initiative, physical/psychic damage) come from "seven base elements." The seventh element may be cosmos itself; unverified.
- **Daemon CDZ RPG:**
  - "Pontos de Cosmo" work like Daemon's Magic Points or Faith Points.
  - Starting characters get 120 attribute points, 350 + INT×5 skill points, and **10 Cosmo Power Points**.
  - Stat blocks list different Cosmo values with armour, without armour, and with the 7th Sense.
  - The Guia das Armaduras adds an "Inflamar Mais o Cosmo" (burn more cosmo) rule.

### Inferences
- **Cosmo is two things in most designs:** a *tier number* (SSL's 100–900, Chasis' Cosmo level, Daemon's Cosmo value) and a *spendable fuel* (ANS points, Daemon Cosmo Points, SSL's "add part of your cosmos to the roll"). The user's plan (Cosmo = number of dice) makes Cosmo the pool size. The designer will still need to decide whether burning Cosmo *spends* dice (resource) or only *sets* them (stat). ANS's "1 point = 2 automatic successes" is a clean conversion rate to borrow.
- **The binary 7th Sense is a cliff.** A bounded system suffers most from an all-or-nothing +X. **Daemon's "both have it, so both lose the bonus"** is a neat way to keep Gold-vs-Gold fights readable, because 7th-Sense bonuses cancel and the base stats decide. The user's "Sense = die size" is closer to a graded ladder (Cortex/Savage-Worlds-style die steps), which avoids the cliff but needs a success-counting rule tuned per die size. In a success-counting pool, a bigger die raises success odds nonlinearly; Paladins' "5 = 1 success, 6 = 2" is one precedent.
- **Cloths:** "armour = bundle of ordinary advantages + one constellation-unique ability" appears independently in Daemon, GURPS, 3DeT Victory and SSL. It maps directly to the user's "each Gold cloth grants a signature attack." SSL's armour-construction rules are a precedent for Bronze/Silver cloths being player-designable while Gold stays a closed list.
- **Techniques:** Daemon's per-effect Cosmo costs (Dano Constante, Redução de Atributo, Testes Difíceis) are a working "point-built technique" model. The Spanish loadout idea (own many, bring only a few into a fight) limits option paralysis and suits the anime's signature-move feel.
- **Speed:** only GURPS models speed explicitly (subsonic → Mach 1–10 → light). The other systems fold light speed into the 7th Sense switch. That suggests speed is better handled as a *tier label tied to Sense* than as a separate stat.
- **Getting back up** is the genre's core beat. The best fan designs make it cost something (ANS willpower) or make PCs non-lethal by default (Paladins).

### Gaps
- **"Same technique doesn't work twice":** none of the sources I saw address it. I could not search the rule texts directly because fetches were blocked. It may exist inside ANS, SSL or Paladins and simply not appear in snippets.
- **Cloth rules left unconfirmed:** cloth *durability, part-by-part damage, repair (Mu / blood repair) and Bronze→Silver→Gold upgrade* rules; the snippets only confirm unique abilities, IP values and armour creation rules.
- **Cosmo mechanics not seen:** how Guardianes de Atenea, Trophy Bronze, IgInss' game and Chevaliers d'Athéna mechanize Cosmo.
- **Dragão Brasil:** how its CDZ material (and the classic 3D&T fan PDFs) statted cloths and techniques.
- **3DeT Victory:** exactly which Advantages Jambô assigned to each cloth tier.

## Q3. What do reviews and forum threads say worked or failed?

### Takeaway
Direct critical discussion is thin in what I could reach (no Reddit, no full forum pages). The praise that is visible concerns **fidelity to the setting's values**:

### Inferences
- **The recurring failure is scope, not dice.** Projects that tried to cover the whole canon (WotG conversion, Chasis game, Daemon's 12+ netbooks) either stalled mid-canon or turned into sprawling bookshelves. The best-received recent designs are small and bespoke: Paladins at 134 pages, and jam games of about 24 pages. For a portfolio project, a tight core (Bronze play plus the Athena, Poseidon, Hades and Asgard frames) is more likely to be finished and read.
- **Class-mapping onto D&D-style games** (the 4E thread) is the approach genre fans find least convincing; the bespoke designs avoid classes entirely. That supports the user's no-class design.
- **Balance needs deliberate canon deviations** (Hypermyth). The designer should say up front where the game departs from canon for playability, for example Gold Saint numbers.

## Q4. Unusual mechanics worth stealing

### Takeaway
The most transferable ideas are:
1. The 7th Sense cancelling itself out when both sides have it (Daemon).
2. Cosmo converting into guaranteed successes (ANS).
3. Heroes only die if deliberately finished off, with damage as attribute attrition (Paladins).
4. Armour destruction and the climax of a fight as triggers for a power-up (Fate).
5. Per-effect Cosmo pricing for techniques (Daemon).
6. A pre-combat technique loadout (Spanish fan system).
7. Flashbacks as a mechanic (Renaissance, Paladins).
8. Roleplay quality lowering difficulty (ANS).
9. Sealing a sense to spike Cosmo (canon, per the Japanese wiki).

### Cited Findings
- **Symmetric 7th-Sense cancel.** Having the 7th Sense makes your combat tests easy, unless your opponent also has it, in which case "both lose the bonuses and fight normally." — [Studocu Daemon rules](https://www.studocu.com/pt-br/document/santo-inacio-loyola-col-est-ens-fund-md/historia/daemon-rpg-sistema-e-regras-dos-cavaleiros-do-zodiaco/168993160)
- **Cosmo → automatic successes; willpower → extra dice or getting back up.**
  - 1 cosmos point = 2 raw successes.
  - 1 willpower point = 2 extra dice, *or* acting past unconsciousness, *or* +5 HP in a crisis.
  - Source: [ANS rules v3.1](http://www.thyamath.fr/Les_chevaliers_du_zodiaque/ANS-Le_Livre_des_regles_v3.1.pdf)
- **Roleplay-driven difficulty.** In ANS, action difficulty is "directly linked to the players' roleplay performance." — [Legrog](https://www.legrog.org/jeux-amateurs/ans)

### Inferences
Applied to the user's proposed design, where Sense level is the die size and Cosmo is the number of dice:
- **ANS's rate as a "burn":** spending Cosmo could turn dice into automatic successes (ANS's 1 → 2 rate). The pool shrinks but becomes reliable, which reads as "burning" Cosmo.
- **Sense-sealing:** voluntarily sealing a sense (canon: Shiryu blinding himself, Shaka closing his eyes) could raise the die size for the rest of the fight at a narrative or mechanical cost. The Japanese wiki line supports this directly.
- **Gold-vs-Gold without number bloat:** Daemon's symmetric cancel could apply to Sense die size. When both combatants share a Sense tier, compare only Cosmo dice. This keeps the numbers bounded.
- **Getting back up:** Paladins' non-lethality combined with ANS's willpower "get back up" (for example, spend a resource to return from knockout with reduced dice) covers the core anime beat.
- **"Same technique doesn't work twice":** no precedent was found, so this is new design territory. One option: once an opponent has *seen and survived* a named technique, it loses dice or its signature effect against that opponent for the rest of the scene. That fits the user's point-built techniques because it pushes players to buy a second technique.
- **Gold cloth signature attacks:** a closed list of 12 fits the precedent of armour = bundle + unique ability (Daemon/GURPS/3DeT). Letting Bronze and Silver cloths be player-designed under rules (as in SSL) keeps customisation where the user's Bronze → Silver ascension happens.
