## 1. Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game (White Wolf, 1994): special maneuvers, Power Points, prerequisites, combos, combat cards, style-based learning

### Takeaway
SF:SG is the closest published ancestor to "named signature attacks bought with points". Special Maneuvers are bought with Power Points (7 at character creation, more via XP). Costs are discounted by fighting **Style**, and prerequisites are set by dot-rated **Techniques** (Punch, Kick, Grab, Athletics, Focus, Block). In play each maneuver is a **Combat Card** with Speed/Damage/Movement modifiers. Players choose cards secretly, announce only the Speed, and faster fighters can interrupt slower ones. Some maneuvers are "modifier" maneuvers that layer on top of basic ones and cost Chi per use.

### Cited Findings
- Maneuvers cost Power Points: "you start with 7 and can get more with experience points." — [RPG Writeups: SF:SG (Halloween Jack)](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/halloween-jack/street-fighter-the-storytelling-game/)
- Style "determines your initial Chi and Willpower, and what Special Maneuvers you can learn, and at what cost. Some Maneuvers can be learned by anyone, but some styles get a break on the Power Point cost." — [RPG Writeups: SF:SG](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/halloween-jack/street-fighter-the-storytelling-game/)
- Example of style discounts: practitioners of Shotokan karate and kung fu could learn the Fireball more easily than someone who picked sambo. — [Wikipedia: SF:SG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter:_The_Storytelling_Game) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Players distribute points among the Punch, Kick, Grab, Athletics, Focus and Block Techniques. These raise combat ability and also serve as **prerequisites** for special maneuvers. — [Wikipedia: SF:SG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter:_The_Storytelling_Game) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Combat round: "the combatants each pick one of their cards and announce the speed of the maneuver (but not anything else, including the move's name)." The book recommends putting each maneuver on a separate Combat Card, forming a deck to choose from each turn. — [RPGnet review of SF:SG](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/9/9871.phtml) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Each move has three traits (speed, movement and damage), computed from the character's traits plus the maneuver's modifiers. Speed is "generally your Dexterity + the Maneuver's Speed Modifier." — [Street Fighter RPG blog: Speed in SF:SG](http://streetfighterrpg.blogspot.com/2010/12/speed-in-street-fighter-storytelling.html); [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/9/9871.phtml) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- The fighter with the lowest speed starts his maneuver first (usually by moving into position), and anyone with higher speed can "interrupt" at any time to perform their own move. — [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/9/9871.phtml) / [SF RPG blog](http://streetfighterrpg.blogspot.com/2010/12/speed-in-street-fighter-storytelling.html) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Modifier maneuver example: **Dragon Claw** adds +1 to speed, damage and movement of any basic punch for **1 Chi**. Its card must be chosen at the start of the turn together with the Basic Punch it modifies. — [RPG PUB thread: "enough detail to distinguish a punch from an elbow strike?"](https://www.rpgpub.com/threads/games-with-enough-detail-to-distinguish-a-punch-from-an-elbow-strike.11783/page-2) (snippet)
- An active fan community still supports the game (FAQ, a "Combat Chart Generator"). — [SFRPG.com FAQ](https://sfrpg.com/street-fighter-storytelling-game-faq/); [SFRPG.com Combat Chart Generator](https://sfrpg.com/combat-chart-generator/)

### Inferences
- "Style → discounted list of maneuvers" maps directly onto Saint Seiya lineages. A constellation or master line (e.g., the Aquarius/Cygnus ice line, the Libra/Dragon Rozan line) could get discounts on thematic techniques, while generic Cosmo moves cost full price for everyone.
- Announcing speed while hiding the maneuver is a built-in "reading" mechanic. The "same technique never works twice" idea could be done as information rather than a number: a defender who has seen technique X learns its name and effect as soon as it is declared, and may pick a defensive card after seeing it. This costs no extra bookkeeping at the table.
- Dragon Claw (a modifier stacked onto a basic move for a per-use Chi cost) is a clean template for evolution. "Pegasus Comet Fist" could be bought as a modifier on "Pegasus Meteor Fist", rather than as a new standalone technique.
- Physical combat cards make each character's technique list visible and tangible. They also make it easy to put markers on cards ("seen by X"), which would support any adaptation rule (see Q5).

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** exact Power Point costs of specific maneuvers per style (e.g., Dragon Punch, Fireball, Hundred Hand Slap), and the table that marks which styles may learn which maneuvers. No fetchable source.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the **combo** system. I recall that combos are fixed sequences of 2–3 maneuvers bought with XP/points, and that "Dizzy combos" auto-dizzy the target if the whole chain lands. My search returned only video-game combo pages ([Street Fighter Wiki: Combo](https://streetfighter.fandom.com/wiki/Combo)), so this is unconfirmed.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Willpower costs of maneuvers, and the Honor/Glory/Renown mechanics.
- No SF:SG rule about diminishing returns for repeated maneuvers was found.

## 2. Mutants & Masterminds 3e: point-buy effects, extras/flaws, Arrays/Alternate Effects, Power Level caps, Power Stunts via Extra Effort

### Takeaway
M&M 3e builds every power from generic **effects** plus **extras** and **flaws**. It balances them with **Power Level (PL) caps that allow trade-offs**: attack bonus + effect rank ≤ 2×PL, and Dodge + Toughness ≤ 2×PL. **Arrays/Alternate Effects** let one pool of points hold several named attacks, usable one at a time. **Extra Effort "power stunts"** let a hero improvise a one-shot alternate effect at the cost of fatigue (which a Hero Point can cancel). The **Removable** flaw (−1 point per 5; −2 per 5 if Easily Removable) is M&M's standard way to price powers that come from an item, such as a Gold cloth.

### Cited Findings
- **Power stunts:** Extra Effort lets you "use powers your PC doesn't have for quick, one-shot situations called 'Power-Stunts'". "The cost of the temporary power cannot exceed the power you're basing it off." — [GiantITP forums: M&M 3e first character](https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-430607.html); see also [d20HeroSRD: Running the Game](https://www.d20herosrd.com/9-gamemastering/8-action-adventure/) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- The cost of Extra Effort is **Fatigue**, and a Hero Point can remove it ("A player may use a hero point to ignore fatigue"). The same snippet claims fatigue "drops your movement −1 rank" and "fades in 10 rounds", which is **uncertain** (see Gaps). — [GiantITP forums](https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-430607.html) (snippet)
- "An alternate effect for an attack works like a use of extra effort for a power stunt, except the character suffers no fatigue." A stunted Alternate Effect "lasts until the end of the scene or until its duration expires", and "Permanent effects cannot be used for power stunts." — [search summary citing d20HeroSRD/forums](https://www.d20herosrd.com/9-gamemastering/8-action-adventure/) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- **Arrays:** the point budget of an Alternate Effect is based on the cost of the array's base power, and "you don't get to add in the cost of other alternate effects." — [Atomic Think Tank: Alternate Effect off of an Array](http://www.atomicthinktank.com/viewtopic.php?f=27&t=46568) (snippet)
- The d20HeroSRD Modifiers page gives the example of "a regular Damage effect and one with the Area modifier" as a typical use of the Alternate Effect modifier. — [d20HeroSRD: Modifiers](https://www.d20herosrd.com/6-powers/modifiers/) (snippet)
- The **Reservoir** extra (a third-party/forum-discussed modifier) adds 1 extra point per rank to spread across Dynamic Alternate Effects. — [Lone Wolf forums: Power Extra Reservoir](https://forums.wolflair.com/threads/power-extra-resavior.60434/) (snippet; likely not core rules)
- **PL caps (trade-off):** attack bonus + effect rank cannot exceed 2×PL. This "does not mean the two traits must themselves be equal." Dodge + Toughness also cannot exceed 2×PL. Example: "Damage 12 is fine for PL10 so long as your attack bonus is +8 or less." — [The Lubrecht Pages: M&M 3e review](https://calubrecht.us/mutantsAndMasterminds/MutantsAndMasterminds3eReview.htm); [RPGGeek: Can advantages break PL limits](https://rpggeek.com/thread/1404952/can-advantages-break-power-level-limits); [Wikipedia: M&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutants_%26_Masterminds) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- **Removable:** "worth –1 point (–2 points for Easily Removable) per 5 total character points of the power's final cost, after applying extras and flaws." Rules example: Mistress Marine's armor costs 98 points; 98 / 5 = 20, so Removable reduces it to 78. — [d20HeroSRD: Modifiers](https://www.d20herosrd.com/6-powers/modifiers/); [d20HeroSRD: Gadgets & Gear](https://www.d20herosrd.com/7-gadgets-gear/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **The PL trade-off rule suits named techniques well.** Pegasus Meteor Fist (accurate, many hits, lower damage per hit) and Excalibur (less accurate, very high damage) can sit under the same cap for the same rank (Bronze/Silver/Gold as "PL tiers"). This gives distinct identities without either outclassing the other.
- **Array = a Saint's repertoire.** A Saint's techniques could be Alternate Effects of one base "Cosmo attack" power, so buying a second or third named technique is cheap. This fits the genre: most Saints fight with 1–3 signature moves. The design question is whether cheap alternates push players to hold many techniques, which would dilute the "signature" feel. A cap on the number of techniques may be needed.
- **Power stunt = "burning Cosmo" to improvise.** In a crisis, a Saint could produce a new variant once, pay fatigue (or a Hero Point analogue such as "Seventh Sense"), and later buy it permanently. This is a direct evolution path for Meteor → Comet (see Q6).
- **Removable = Gold cloth technique.** The cloth-granted signature attack can be priced as a power with the Removable flaw. In Saint Seiya, cloths are removed, destroyed, stolen and refuse unworthy wearers, which justifies the discount. With M&M numbers, a 20-point cloth technique would cost 16 (Removable) or 12 (Easily Removable).

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** exact array pricing. My recollection is that each Alternate Effect costs a flat 1 point and each Dynamic Alternate Effect a flat 2 points, and that Damage costs 1 PP per rank. The search summary did not confirm these numbers.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the **Fades** flaw (I recall −1 per rank; the effect loses 1 rank each time it is used). This is a core-rules modifier for "loses effectiveness when used" and is worth checking. The same goes for **Unreliable** and **Limited**.
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether M&M 3e **Immunity** can be bought against a specific power or descriptor (e.g., "Immunity to Diamond Dust"), which would model permanent adaptation.
- Fatigue details (hindered/speed penalty; recovery time) conflict with my recollection and are unverified.

## 3. BESM (Big Eyes Small Mouth) 3e/4e: Special Attack attribute with Abilities/Disabilities and levels

### Takeaway
BESM builds attacks as a **leveled Attribute**, reported at 4 points per level and 15 damage per level for its damage attribute (edition uncertain). The attack is then customized with enhancements ("Abilities") and limitations ("Disabilities"). Beyond that, I could not verify the 3e/4e specifics, but the model is simple enough to be a strong candidate for a Saint Seiya technique builder.

### Cited Findings
- "Weapon Attack in BESM costs 4 points per level and gives an attack that does 15 damage per level." — [RPG Writeups: BESM (WhitemageofDOOM)](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/whitemageofdoom/big-eyes-small-mouth/) (snippet; **edition not specified**: "Weapon Attack" was the 2e name, so this may be 2e)
- "Special Attack is an attribute that is a point buy system for creating attacks that do more than just damage." — [RPG Writeups: BESM](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/whitemageofdoom/big-eyes-small-mouth/) / [RPG with Kaworu: BESM 3e review](https://rpgwithkaworu.wordpress.com/2022/10/02/besm-3rd-edition-a-review/) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Damage: add Combat Value plus applicable Attribute bonuses to the attack's base damage. — [BESM Rules wikidot: Combat](http://besm-rules.wikidot.com/combat) (snippet)
- BESM 3e reviews exist on RPGnet and a 2022 blog review. — [RPGnet review of BESM 3e](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/12/12712.phtml); [RPG with Kaworu](https://rpgwithkaworu.wordpress.com/2022/10/02/besm-3rd-edition-a-review/)

### Inferences
- The generic BESM pattern is: buy N levels; each extra property costs part of the damage; each drawback gives some back. It is probably the **lowest-cognitive-load** way to let players build named attacks. For CDZ: a technique has N levels; adding "multi-hit (Meteor)", "area (Galaxian Explosion)", "freezing (Diamond Dust)", "ignores armor (Excalibur)" or "defensive/rebound (Rozan Shield/Nebula Chain)" each costs a level. Drawbacks such as "needs a charge-up pose", "costs extra Cosmo", "only with cloth" or "cannot be used twice in a row" each give a level back. That last one is itself an anti-repetition mechanic built into the technique, not the game.
- Letting "cannot be repeated consecutively" or "loses 1 level each reuse in the scene" be a **player-chosen Disability** puts the anti-repetition bookkeeping only on techniques whose owners were paid to take it.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED (general knowledge):** BESM renamed its attack attribute across editions (2e "Weapon Attack"/"Special Attack", 3e "Special Attack", 4e/Dyskami 2018 "Weapon"). I recall that in 2e/3e each Ability reduces damage by one level and each Disability adds one level (abilities such as Area Effect, Auto-Fire, Penetrating, Stun, Spreading, Linked; disabilities such as Limited Shots, Uses Energy, Unreliable, Slow, Short Range, Self-Destruct). Could not confirm the exact list or cost ratios for 3e or 4e.
- The 4e damage per level and point costs are unverified.

## 4. Wushu, Feng Shui 2, L5R 5e, Anima, Hero System, Cortex Prime SFX, Fate Core stunts (plus Legends of the Wulin)

### Takeaway
These systems cover a spectrum. At the narrative end, **Wushu** has no technique purchase at all: the dice come from description, and a group veto stops boring descriptions. **Fate** stunts are cheap and templated, and **Cortex** SFX are powered by spending a resource. In the middle, **Feng Shui** fu schticks carry per-use Chi and shot costs, **L5R 5e** gates techniques by school rank, and **Anima** uses Martial Knowledge with degree-based martial arts. At the crunchy end, **Hero System** prices Active vs Real points, and its martial maneuvers are bought at 3–5 points each. For a Saint Seiya game, the most transferable ideas are: per-use resource costs, **cost in time** (FS shots), **rank gating**, and **Exalted-style named "signatures"** (covered in Q5–Q7).

### Cited Findings
**Wushu (Daniel Bayn)**
- "Wushu is based on the Principle of Narrative Truth: everything a player describes, happens. The roll of dice only shows how those actions advanced the story." — [Wushu: Playing the Game (danielbayn.com)](https://danielbayn.com/wushu/playing-the-game/index.shtml) (snippet)
- "each discrete element within the narrative is a Detail which earns a die... dialog is a Detail, an action is a Detail, the environment... your character's wardrobe is a Detail." — [Philosophy of Games: Wushu overview](http://philosophyofgames.blogspot.com/2008/12/wushu-overviewreview.html) / [danielbayn.com](https://danielbayn.com/wushu/playing-the-game/index.shtml) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- **Veto:** "Anyone at the table, gamemaster or player, can veto any detail and ask the player to describe something different," which keeps tone and genre consistent. — [RPGnet review of Wushu Open](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/12/12398.phtml) / [Philosophy of Games](http://philosophyofgames.blogspot.com/2008/12/wushu-overviewreview.html) (snippet; attribution approximate)

**Feng Shui (fu schticks; note that the fetched sources are 1st-edition fan pages, not FS2)**
- Fan-compiled fu powers list **Chi cost** and **Shot cost** per use. Examples: a power at Chi 1 / Shot 0 gives +2 Dodge until end of shot; a bare-handed martial arts attack at Chi 1 / Shot 3 deals +2 damage and sets the opponent burning for 1 damage per 3 shots. — [Flick Inc: Single Fu Powers](http://www.flick.com/games/RPGs/fengshui/fupower/singlefu.html) (fan site, snippet)
- Fan "double fu" combinations of two schticks, e.g., "Flying Windmill Kick" at Chi 7 / Shot 5. — [Flick Inc: Double Fu Combinations](http://www.flick.com/games/RPGs/fengshui/fupower/doublefu.html) (fan site, snippet)
- House rule: Magic Weapon schticks, available to characters with the Signature Weapon schtick, count as fu schticks costing "3 + x" (x = number of fu schticks you'll end up with) but mostly have no Chi/Shot costs. — [innocence.com: Magic Weapon Schticks (house rules)](https://innocence.com/fengshui/house-rules/magic-weapon-schticks.html) (house rule, snippet)
- FS2 exists with official errata from Atlas Games. — [Atlas Games: Feng Shui 2 Errata](https://www.atlas-games.com/fengshui/fengshui2errata)

**Legend of the Five Rings 5e (FFG)**
- There are seven technique categories. Most characters use kata (martial), shuji (social) and rituals. Shugenja use invocations, monks kihō, shinobi ninjutsu, and fallen characters mahō. — [Strange Assembly: L5R 5e review](https://www.strangeassembly.com/2018/review-legend-of-the-five-rings-fifth-edition-by-fantasy-flight-games)
- "Kata are the techniques of bushi and others skilled in the use of weapons." Kihō are "complex martial arts techniques learned by monks and sometimes shugenja that have some extraordinary or supernatural effect. Bushi cannot learn kiho." — [FFG: A Masterful Technique](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2018/9/11/a-masterful-technique/) / [Strange Assembly](https://www.strangeassembly.com/2018/review-legend-of-the-five-rings-fifth-edition-by-fantasy-flight-games) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Rank-gating example: the kata "Breath of Wind Style" requires School Rank 3. — [FFG forum archive: Stances, Kata, Maneuvers](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/199086-rpg-combat-stances-kata-maneuvers-and-weapons/) / [Phil's Let's Study L5R Part 4](https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2018/11/12/lets-study-legend-of-the-five-rings-part-4-schools-and-techniques/) (snippet; attribution approximate)

**Anima: Beyond Fantasy**
- **Martial Knowledge (MK)** points "can only be used toward acquiring Dominion knowledge such as acquiring Ki Abilities and special Techniques". MK cannot buy Ki points or Ki Accumulation. Accumulation is the rate at which Ki is gathered each turn to unleash Dominion techniques. — [Anima wiki: Ki Dominion](https://animarpgbeyondfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Ki_Dominion); [FFG forum archive: Ki and Martial Knowledge](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/73849-ki-and-martial-knowledge/) (snippet)
- Martial arts have degrees: three for basic arts (Base → Advanced → Supreme) and two for advanced arts. You must learn Base first and meet requirements before advancing. — [Anima wiki: Martial Arts](https://animarpgbeyondfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Martial_Arts)
- MK is gained by level via each class's MK progression, and also through the Martial Mastery advantage or by mastering some martial arts. — [Anima wiki: Martial Mastery](https://animarpgbeyondfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Martial_Mastery) (snippet)

**Hero System (the original point-build)**
- Base cost + Advantages = **Active Cost**, which drives range, END cost and activation difficulty. Limitations are then applied to give **Real Points**, the price actually paid. Example: Energy Blast 8d6 = 40 Active Points; with the OAF (Obvious Accessible Focus) limitation (−1) it costs 20 Real Points. — [Wikipedia: Hero System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_System) (snippet)
- **Martial maneuvers:** "no single maneuver can cost more than 5 points or less than 3." Offensive Strike (usually a kick) is built as +4d6 (6 points), −2 OCV (−2) and +1 DCV (+1), for a total of 5 points. — [RPGnet review of Hero System Martial Arts](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/14/14788.phtml); [HERO 5e: Franklin's House Rules for Martial Arts](https://fcain.tripod.com/h5skills/martial_arts.html) (snippet; the "+4d6 = 6 points" and "DC × 5" breakdown appears to come from **Franklin's house rules**, not RAW)
- In that breakdown, a strike maneuver's base cost equals its total DC × 5. Each maneuver has at least one "Basis" (e.g., Strike), and most are "Exclusive". — [Franklin's House Rules](https://fcain.tripod.com/h5skills/martial_arts.html) (house rule, snippet)
- A 5e list of standard HTH maneuvers with costs is available. — [HERO 5e: List of Standard HTH/Melee Maneuvers](https://fcain.tripod.com/h5skills/martial_arts_melee.html)

**Cortex Prime (SFX, signature assets)** (source is a 2018 *draft* of the Game Handbook)
- Players "may not step up assets without a distinction or signature asset SFX that explicitly permits them to do so". They can spend an additional PP to make an asset last the rest of the session; otherwise it vanishes at the end of the scene. — [Cortex Prime Game Handbook draft 2018-06-28](https://dicehaven.com/wp-content/uploads/CP_GameHandbook_2018-06-28_CH1_CH4.pdf)
- Good signature-asset SFX let the player "spend 1 PP to step up a skill when the signature asset is used". Rule of thumb: "no more than three SFX to be attached to a signature asset, in total, with one being the most common number." — [Cortex Prime draft handbook](https://dicehaven.com/wp-content/uploads/CP_GameHandbook_2018-06-28_CH1_CH4.pdf)

**Fate Core (stunts)**
- Three stunts are free, and up to two more cost 1 refresh each. Refresh starts at 3 and can never go below 1. Rationale: "the more cool tricks you can do, the more you'll need to accept compels." — [Fate Core SRD: Stunts & Refresh](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/stunts-refresh); [Fate SRD Brasil (PT)](https://fatesrdbrasil.gitlab.io/fate-srd/fate-core/stunts-refresh/)
- The Fate System Toolkit has a dedicated chapter on alternative stunt costs. — [Fate System Toolkit: Stunt Costs](https://fate-srd.com/fate-system-toolkit/stunt-costs); [PT version](https://fatesrdbrasil.gitlab.io/fate-srd/fate-system-toolkit/stunt-costs/)

**Legends of the Wulin (Eos Press; Weapons of the Gods successor)**
- External Kung Fu styles have strengths and weaknesses that give passive benefits and require no Chi. Chi is recovered at the end of each round depending on Rank, and is spent on Internal Kung Fu techniques and Chi Aura. — [TV Tropes: Legends of the Wulin](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TabletopGame/LegendsOfTheWulin); [RPG Writeups: LotW](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/dareon/legends-of-the-wulin/) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Elemental Chi (the snippet lists Water, Earth, Fire, Air, Metal and Wood; the element list should be checked against the book) has a **2-for-1** effect when spent on techniques of the matching element. — [TV Tropes / Sufficient Velocity thread](https://forums.sufficientvelocity.com/threads/legends-of-the-wulin-weapons-of-the-gods-with-better-mechanics.118833/) (snippet)
- Roll modifiers come from these categories: Skills, Equipment, External KF, Internal KF, "Laughs at and Fears", Lores, Chi Conditions, Secret Arts techniques. Archetype determines which Secret Arts you can access and which skill regenerates extra Chi. — [RPG Writeups: LotW](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/dareon/legends-of-the-wulin/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Per-use cost types are the main balancing lever** across these systems: Chi (FS, LotW, Anima Ki), time/shots (FS), refresh/fate points (Fate), plot points (Cortex), END (Hero). Saint Seiya already has a canonical per-use resource (**Cosmo**), and a natural "time" cost (the charge-up pose and naming the attack aloud).
- **Element-matched discounts** (LotW's 2-for-1 on aligned techniques) map directly onto CDZ elements and constellations (ice for Camus/Hyoga, fire for Aiolia/Ikki's Phoenix, and so on).
- **Rank gating** (L5R "school rank 3") and **degree progression** (Anima Base → Advanced → Supreme) map onto Bronze → Silver → Gold, and onto Sixth → Seventh → Eighth Sense.
- **Fate's "refresh" trade-off** is a useful philosophy: more signature techniques should mean fewer baseline meta-currency points. Similarly, an extra named technique could reduce a Saint's starting "Cosmo burn" or "Miracle" points.
- **Hero's Active vs Real points** idea: rank caps should limit a technique's *effective power* (Active), while *price* (Real) varies with limitations. This stops "cloth-only" or "needs pose" drawbacks from buying a Bronze an attack beyond their tier.
- **Wushu's veto** shows the narrative route to anti-repetition: the group can refuse a description that is merely "I do Pegasus Meteor Fist again". It costs no bookkeeping but depends on table culture.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** FS2 specifics (shot counter start and descending sequence, 3-shot standard attack, fu "paths", whether FS2 kept Chi or merged it into Fortune). The only fetched material was 1st-edition fan pages.
- **UNVERIFIED:** L5R 5e technique XP costs (I recall 3 XP per technique rank, with curriculum rules affecting rank progress) and the effects of the five stances (Air/Earth/Fire/Water/Void).
- **UNVERIFIED:** Anima Ki technique levels (1/2/3), MK costs per level, and the "disadvantages" that reduce a technique's MK cost.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Fate stunt templates (+2 to a skill in a narrow circumstance; use skill A in place of B; once-per-session rule-breaking effect).
- **UNVERIFIED:** Hero System Multipower/Variable Slot costs and the Focus limitation values (OAF −1, OIF −½, etc., by recollection).
- **UNVERIFIED:** Weapons of the Gods / Legends of the Wulin "Lore" or "Loresheet" mechanics (how Lores grant techniques, secret arts or styles, and their costs). The only confirmation is that "Lores" appear as a modifier category ([RPG Writeups: LotW](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/dareon/legends-of-the-wulin/)). How they work was not verified.

## 5. Adaptation and diminishing returns on repeated moves: existing mechanics, and how to tune them without bookkeeping overload

### Takeaway
No tabletop source found in this session has a direct "opponent gains a cumulative bonus against a technique it has seen" rule. The closest analogues come from video and card games, and they fall into five families, from least to most bookkeeping:
- **(a)** consecutive-use penalties, where only the last move matters (Pokémon Protect/Torment)
- **(b)** cooldowns (BattleCON)
- **(c)** escalating self-cost per use (Lancer Overcharge)
- **(d)** a recency/frequency queue (Smash Bros stale-move negation; DMC style decay)
- **(e)** persistent per-enemy immunities learned across encounters (Shadow of Mordor Nemesis; MGSV Enemy Preparedness)

Exalted 2e's combo "Signature" (a visual look "instantly recognizable to anyone who's seen it before") is the nearest tabletop hook for tracking "has this character seen this move?".

### Cited Findings
- **Smash Bros stale-move negation:** uses "a queue of the last nine moves a character has connected with". A repeated move's damage drops based on how often it appears in the queue *and* how recently it was used (a move from 2 moves ago is weaker than one from 7 or 8 moves ago). A queue filled with one move gives **55%** of original damage (maximum staleness). Since Brawl, a move not in the queue at all gets a small **freshness bonus**. — [SmashWiki: Stale-move negation](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Stale-move_negation); [Smashpedia](https://supersmashbros.fandom.com/wiki/Stale-Move_Negation) (snippet)
- **Pokémon Protect/Detect:** using it twice in a row makes the second attempt 1/3 likely to succeed, then 1/9, and so on (100% → 33% → 11% → 3.7%). **Torment** prevents the target from using the same move twice in a row until it switches out. — [Bulbapedia: Protect](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Protect_(move)); [Bulbapedia: Torment](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Torment_(move)); [PokéBase answer](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/117476/what-is-the-chance-of-using-protect-twice-in-a-row) (snippet; generation differences not verified)
- **Devil May Cry Stylish Rank:** "Performing the same moves repeatedly will see diminishing returns on the boost." The rank depletes through inactivity "or by continuously using the same attacks without much variation", and "the higher the current grade of Style, the faster the gauge will deplete." — [DMC Wiki: Stylish Rank](https://devilmaycry.fandom.com/wiki/Stylish_Rank); [Shacknews: Stylish Points in DMC5](https://www.shacknews.com/article/110322/stylish-points-and-style-ranks-in-devil-may-cry-5) (snippet)
- **BattleCON (Level 99 Games, card fighting game) cooldown:** at the end of each beat, the Style and Base cards played go to a cooldown area with two slots. When you play a new pair, the oldest pair returns to hand, so "any card you play, you will not see either piece of that combo again for two turns." A **Style** is character-specific, while **Bases** (heavy punch, light punch, projectile, throw…) are mostly shared by all characters. — [Steam guide: Beginner's Primer to BattleCON](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1447481784); [BattleCON Print-and-Play rules v1.3](https://tesera.ru/images/items/65661/BattleCON-print-and-play-2.pdf) (snippet)
- **Lancer (Massif Press) Overcharge:** the heat cost escalates with each use: 1 Heat, then 1d3, then 1d6, then 1d6+4, resetting only at the next Full Repair. — [LANCER Rules (carrd)](https://lancer-rules.carrd.co/); [Cheatography: Lancer Mech Combat](https://cheatography.com/stiergott/cheat-sheets/lancer-rpg-mech-combat/pdf_bw/?last=1746760670); [Train Lightning: Overcharge Overhaul](https://trainlightning.com/overcharge-overhaul/) (snippet)
- **Shadow of Mordor/War Nemesis system:** an orc captain "that you defeated in battle – but didn't execute – may return immune to the bow that shot him", and others "may become immune to your stealth attacks if you killed them from the shadows." Immunities and weaknesses change over time as orcs fight, cheat death or get shamed. Players can learn them in advance by gathering intel (from "Worm" informants in Shadow of War). — [GamesRadar: Nemesis system](https://www.gamesradar.com/shadow-mordor-nemesis-system-amazing-how-works/); [Shadow of War wiki: Nemesis](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Nemesis); [TheGamer: Nemesis guide](https://www.thegamer.com/middle-earth-shadow-of-war-orc-nemesis-system-complete-guide/) (snippet)
- **Metal Gear Solid V "Revenge"/Enemy Preparedness:** enemies adopt counters to your playstyle: many headshots → helmets; repeated night infiltration → night-vision goggles; sneaking → more mines; going loud → more heavy-armor troops. Levels drop when you complete missions without the triggering behaviour, and destroying supply lines cuts off the countermeasure. — [Metal Gear Wiki: Revenge System](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Revenge_System_(enemy_preparedness)); [Konami support: Enemy Preparedness](https://eu-support.konami.com/hc/en-gb/articles/9667929703191-The-Phantom-Pain-Enemy-Preparedness); [Steam discussion: Enemies adapting](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/527274088393900126/) (snippet)
- **Exalted 2e Combos:** "each combo has a distinct Signature—a look which is instantly recognizable to anyone who's seen it before." Each combo also needs a Name. — [RPGnet wiki: Exalted Combos](https://wiki.rpg.net/index.php/Exalted_Combos) (snippet; this may be fan-wiki text rather than rulebook text)
- **Exalted perfect defenses (the "can't be hit by it" end of the spectrum):** Heavenly Guardian Defense is described as a parry that "perfectly defends against any attack, even if it is unblockable". It can guard against normally unparriable damage "for just four motes", and has a 4 motes + 1 Willpower use against uncountable recurring damage. Forum threads show ongoing debate over how it resets. — [Onyx Path forums: How does HGD work?](https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/exalted/909538-how-does-heavenly-guardian-defense-work/page2); [Exalted Unofficial Wiki: HGD discussion](http://exalted.xi.co.nz/wiki/Discussions/HeavenlyGuardianDefence) (snippet; **edition mixing likely**: 2e and 3e wording differ, so do not rely on the numbers without the book)

### Inferences
Analysis of the proposed rule ("a target who has seen a technique gains a cumulative bonus against it"):
- **Bookkeeping cost:** a full "who has seen what, how many times" matrix grows as opponents × techniques × exposures. In a Twelve Houses arc with 5 Bronzes vs 12 Golds this becomes heavy. The fixes below are ranked from lightest to heaviest.
  1. **Binary "Seen" flag instead of a cumulative count** (Nemesis-style immunity, MGSV tiers). The first exposure costs the attacker nothing. Once seen, the defender gets a fixed bonus (e.g., +2 defense, or one step of dice) against that technique for the rest of the scene. Track it with one mark per technique on the defender's sheet. PCs track their own marks (they are motivated to), and the GM tracks only named NPCs. Mooks never adapt.
  2. **Cap the stack.** If cumulative, use +1 per exposure up to +3. Smash's queue caps staleness at 55% damage, which is effectively a floor. An unbounded bonus eventually produces immunity, which kills one-technique characters (most Bronzes early in the story).
  3. **Last-used-only rule** (Pokémon Protect/Torment). Only the immediately previous technique matters: e.g., repeating the same technique on consecutive turns against the same target is at −2 or halves damage. This needs no tracking and is cheap to explain, but it does not capture "seen once, forever".
  4. **Cooldown** (BattleCON): a technique returns only after 2 rounds. It works well if techniques are physical cards (as in SF:SG), but it is a restriction on the user rather than adaptation by the enemy.
  5. **Escalating self-cost** (Lancer): each reuse in a scene costs more Cosmo (e.g., +1, +d3, +d6). One track per character, not per pair. It models exhaustion, not "the enemy has read it".
  6. **Campaign-level adaptation** (Nemesis/MGSV): recurring villains come back with a counter to what beat them last time. This is GM-side only, with zero table bookkeeping, and fits "the same technique never works twice on a Saint" between arcs.
- **Flip the sign to cut bookkeeping:** Smash's freshness bonus and DMC's variety reward suggest giving a *bonus for techniques the target has not yet seen* (or a bonus for not repeating the last technique), rather than a penalty for seen ones. In play it amounts to the same thing, and it pushes players toward the canonical behaviour (switching techniques, revealing new ones, evolving).
- **Built-in escape valves** (keep the genre intact):
  - (a) **Evolution resets "Seen"**: Meteor → Comet counts as a new technique, which is exactly the canon.
  - (b) **Spending a big resource** (burning Cosmo, Seventh Sense) ignores the adaptation for one use, like M&M's Hero Point cancelling fatigue.
  - (c) **Only "Saints" adapt**: canon applies the rule to Saints, so soldiers, Specters and mooks might not, or only above a rank. This limits tracking to named combatants.
  - (d) **Reveal instead of a bonus**: as in SF:SG, a defender who has seen the technique may pick its defensive response after the attack is declared. That is information, not numbers.
- **Observer rule:** decide whether watching an ally get hit counts as "seeing". In canon, Saints often learn by watching (e.g., a technique analysed after it hits someone else). A light version: observers get the flag only if they spend an action to "study" it. This also rewards a tactical "analyse" action.

### Gaps
- **No tabletop rule found** (in this session's limited search) that directly implements "opponent adapts to your repeated technique". Candidates I could not check: GURPS Martial Arts, Tenra Bansho Zero, Weapons of the Gods/Legends of the Wulin (Chi Conditions), Exalted 3e (onslaught penalty, which works in the opposite direction), Burning Wheel "Fight!", and existing Saint Seiya fan RPGs.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Burning Wheel's "Let It Ride" rule (a test isn't re-rolled unless circumstances change), a possible narrative analogue.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Tony Hawk's Pro Skater trick degradation (repeated tricks score less within a combo or session); fighting-game combo "damage scaling"/"hitstun deterioration"; Killer Instinct "Combo Breaker" reading repeated patterns. Search budget ran out before checking.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Final Fantasy "enemy learns" behaviours. The known FF mechanic is **Blue Magic**, where the *player* learns enemy moves, which is the reverse direction. The Darkest Dungeon "guarding" and Fire Emblem repetition mechanics named in the brief could not be checked, and I know of no Darkest Dungeon mechanic that penalizes repetition. **Honey Heist** (a one-page game) has no adaptation mechanic as far as I know (unverified).
- Pokémon generation differences for the Protect penalty (older generations used 1/2 per consecutive use, by recollection) are unverified.

## 6. Technique evolution/upgrades (a move growing into a stronger version)

### Takeaway
Systems handle evolution in four ways:
- **(a)** **Extending an existing named package** by paying for new components (Exalted 2e Combos).
- **(b)** **Degree/rank tiers** of the same art (Anima Base → Advanced → Supreme; L5R rank-gated kata).
- **(c)** **Improvise first, buy later** (M&M power stunts via Extra Effort).
- **(d)** **Items that grow with the wielder** (Exalted 3e Evocations; Scion Relics adding Purviews with dots; see Q7).

SF:SG's modifier maneuvers (Dragon Claw on a Basic Punch) give a fifth model: the upgrade as a stacking add-on.

### Cited Findings
- **Exalted 2e:** "Adding a Charm to an existing Combo costs experience points equal to the minimum Ability requirement of the Charm to be added, with an equal training time measured in days." A combo needs "a cool name". — [RPGnet wiki: Exalted Combos](https://wiki.rpg.net/index.php/Exalted_Combos); [Exalted 2.75e wikidot: Charms and Combos](http://exalted275e.wikidot.com/charms-and-combos) (2.75e is a fan patch; snippet; attribution approximate)
- Some Exalted groups house-rule that combos cost no XP. — [Teflonshugenja House Rules (Exalted Unofficial Wiki)](http://exalted.xi.co.nz/wiki/Teflonshugenja/HouseRules); [GiantITP: Exalted 2 Combo understanding](https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-581514.html) (snippet)
- **Anima:** basic martial arts have three degrees (Base, Advanced, Supreme), and you must meet requirements before advancing. — [Anima wiki: Martial Arts](https://animarpgbeyondfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Martial_Arts)
- **L5R 5e:** kata such as Breath of Wind Style require School Rank 3. — [FFG forum archive](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/199086-rpg-combat-stances-kata-maneuvers-and-weapons/) (snippet)
- **M&M 3e:** Extra Effort power stunts create temporary alternate effects, no costlier than the base power, at the cost of fatigue. — [GiantITP forums](https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-430607.html) (snippet)
- **SF:SG:** Dragon Claw modifies any basic punch (+1 speed/damage/move for 1 Chi), and both cards are selected together. — [RPG PUB thread](https://www.rpgpub.com/threads/games-with-enough-detail-to-distinguish-a-punch-from-an-elbow-strike.11783/page-2) (snippet)
- **Exalted 3e artifacts** "grow in power alongside your character, unlocking 'Evocations'". — [It Came From The Bookshelf: Arms of the Chosen](http://www.itcamefromthebookshelf.com/2025/12/exalted-3e-arms-of-chosen.html) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Recommended CDZ pattern ("Evolved Technique"):** buy the evolution as an upgrade whose price is the *difference* between the old and new technique budgets (Exalted's pay-to-extend model). It inherits the original's name root (Meteor → Comet), and **counts as unseen** for adaptation purposes. This makes evolution the in-fiction answer to "never works twice".
- **Trigger evolution through play, not just XP:** M&M-style improvisation lets a Saint in extremis produce the evolved form once (Cosmo burn plus fatigue) and later formalize it with XP. This mirrors canon, where evolutions appear in desperate moments.
- **Tie evolution tiers to rank/sense gates** (Anima degrees, L5R ranks): e.g., the "Supreme" form requires the Seventh Sense.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED (search budget exhausted):** **Legend of Dragoon** "Additions" level up after a number of uses and gain damage/SP, a clean "mastery through use" evolution model. **Romancing SaGa** "glimmer/spark" lets characters learn new, stronger techniques mid-battle, often derived from a technique they are using. **Kingdom Hearts** and **Octopath Traveler** also have mastery-unlock mechanics. All are relevant but uncited.
- Exact Exalted 2e combo prerequisites (e.g., same-Ability restrictions, Essence minimum) are unverified.

## 7. Signature items granting a move (Exalted artifacts, Scion Birthrights, Godbound artifacts, etc.)

### Takeaway
There are three mature models:
- **(1) Exalted 3e Evocations:** an artifact (3+ dots) unlocks item-specific Charms for 10 XP each, usable only while attuned. Resonance with the wielder makes them better, dissonance worse.
- **(2) Scion 2e Relics:** a Relic's dot rating buys Purviews and/or a unique Relic Knack, usable while the item is on hand, plus an Enhancement bonus.
- **(3) Point-cost discount for removability:** M&M Removable (−1/5, −2/5 if easy), Hero System Focus (e.g., an OAF halves cost: 40 Active → 20 Real).

Cortex adds a practical cap: 1–3 SFX per signature asset, with one the norm. Godbound lets mortals reach god-tier power through artifacts "at a price".

### Cited Findings
- **Exalted 3e Evocations:** artifacts "grow in power alongside your character, unlocking 'Evocations'". Starting at three dots ("where most Artifact weapons are"), artifacts can produce Evocations, which "cost 10xp (or one Charm slot) to unlock". They "function for an attuned user like Charms, but usually specifically involve that Artifact." — [It Came From The Bookshelf: Arms of the Chosen](http://www.itcamefromthebookshelf.com/2025/12/exalted-3e-arms-of-chosen.html); [Octopoid Prevarication: Artifacts and Evocations](https://tentacledvitriol.wordpress.com/2016/05/20/artifacts-and-evocations/) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- **Exalted 3e resonance:** there are three states (full resonance, neutral, dissonance), expressed as "resonant"/"dissonant" keywords on Evocations. A blogger argues dissonant is so bad and resonant so good that players nearly always pick resonant artifacts. — [Octopoid Prevarication](https://tentacledvitriol.wordpress.com/2016/05/20/artifacts-and-evocations/) (opinion; snippet)
- **Scion 2e Relics:** "A two-dot Relic can grant one Purview. Add two dots to the Relic's rating for a second Purview. Add three dots... for a third Purview; to do so, you must offset the dot value with Flaws. Relics cannot have more than 3 Purviews." A Relic may provide a unique **Knack**, which the Scion can access "anytime she has the item on hand"; depending on the Knack she may need to use the Relic or merely carry it. The Relic's Enhancement also applies to rolls to activate Boons in its Purviews. — [Scion 2e Wiki: Birthrights](https://scion-origin.fandom.com/wiki/Birthrights); [Scion 2e Wiki: Birthright](https://scion-origin.fandom.com/wiki/Birthright) (snippet)
- **Godbound:** Words grant powers (Gifts). **Effort** is spent or **committed** to invoke gifts and miracles. "A mortal hero can use artifacts to gain Godbound levels of power, but not without a price." Only Artificers can make artifacts based on other Words. — [RPG Writeups: Godbound](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/doresh/godbound/); [TV Tropes: Godbound](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TabletopGame/Godbound) (snippet)
- **M&M 3e Removable:** −1 point per 5 (Easily Removable −2 per 5) of the final cost. Worked example: 98 → 78. — [d20HeroSRD: Modifiers](https://www.d20herosrd.com/6-powers/modifiers/)
- **Hero System Focus:** Energy Blast 8d6 is 40 Active Points; with OAF (−1) it costs 20 Real Points. — [Wikipedia: Hero System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_System) (snippet)
- **Cortex Prime signature assets:** SFX commonly let you spend 1 PP to step up when the asset is used. Aim for at most 3 SFX per signature asset, 1 being most common. — [Cortex Prime draft handbook (2018)](https://dicehaven.com/wp-content/uploads/CP_GameHandbook_2018-06-28_CH1_CH4.pdf)
- **Feng Shui (house rule):** Magic Weapon schticks tied to the Signature Weapon schtick count as fu schticks with an escalating cost (3 + x). — [innocence.com house rules](https://innocence.com/fengshui/house-rules/magic-weapon-schticks.html) (house rule)

### Inferences
- **Gold cloth technique, recommended structure (a synthesis of Evocations and Scion Relic Knacks):**
  - The cloth grants **one signature technique** (e.g., the Aquarius cloth grants Aurora Execution), usable only while the cloth is worn. This works like a Scion Relic Knack "on hand" and an Exalted "attuned user".
  - **Resonance:** a wearer whose constellation or element matches the cloth (Hyōga wearing Aquarius) uses it at full power or unlocks further "evocations". A non-matching wearer (Seiya in Sagittarius) gets a weaker or limited version. This follows Exalted's resonant/dissonant keywords.
  - **Growth:** additional cloth techniques unlock for XP over time (Exalted's 10 XP per Evocation), so the cloth "grows with" the Saint. This matches canon, where cloths respond to Cosmo and blood-revival.
  - **Cost accounting, three options:** (a) free, but the cloth is a Birthright-like background with a dot price; (b) the technique is bought with a Removable-style discount (M&M −20% or −40%); (c) no discount, but the technique doesn't count against the character's own technique cap. Option (c) is simplest if Gold cloths are story rewards rather than purchases.
  - **Cap:** follow Cortex's rule of thumb and give 1 signature technique per cloth (up to 3 for legendary cloths like Libra's weapons). That keeps "signature" meaningful.
- **Interaction with adaptation (Q5):** decide whether "seen" is tracked per technique or per technique+user. If the Aquarius technique is seen when Camus uses it, does it count as seen when Hyōga uses it in the Aquarius cloth? Canon usually says a different user's Cosmo makes it effectively new. That argues for per-user tracking, or for letting evolution/resonance reset it.

- **UNVERIFIED:** Godbound artifact creation costs (Dominion/Influence) and the exact "price" for mortals.

This could be the most directly relevant prior art and is worth a separate search when budget allows.
