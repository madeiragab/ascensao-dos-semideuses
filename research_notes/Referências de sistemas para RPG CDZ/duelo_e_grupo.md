## 1. Duel subsystems: what makes one-on-one duels tense (hidden simultaneous choices, bidding, reading the opponent)?

### Takeaway
Tense duel systems share a small set of techniques. (a) **Hidden simultaneous commitment**: scripted or face-down actions revealed together (Mouse Guard, Burning Wheel, En Garde!, Street Fighter RPG, Riddle of Steel's attack/defend call). (b) **A finite pool split across attack and defense within an exchange**, so every die spent on offense leaves you exposed (Riddle of Steel). (c) **Momentum that builds and then is cashed out**: build a resource, then gamble it on one decisive blow (Exalted 3e withering to decisive; L5R Focus to Free Raises to Strike). (d) **Information warfare**: assessing the opponent before committing (L5R Assessment, the L5R 5e Air/"Predict" option). (e) **Self-inflicted pressure**, where you bid your own composure or stress for tempo and risk breaking (L5R 5e Staredown strife bids into Compromised/Unmask). (f) **Anti-spam variety rules**, where you can't repeat a maneuver (7th Sea 2e). Saint Seiya's "burn your Cosmo higher than the enemy" beat maps most directly onto (e) plus (c): a secret bid of a resource that both powers you and threatens to break you.

### Cited Findings

**Legend of the Five Rings, 4th edition (iaijutsu duel: Assessment, Focus, Strike)**
- Assessment: each duelist "assumes the Center Stance" and rolls "Iaijutsu (Assessment) / Awareness" vs **TN 10 + opponent's Insight Rank × 5**. Success reveals one piece of information, plus one more per Raise: opponent's Void, Reflexes, Iaijutsu skill, Iaijutsu emphases, current Void Points, or current Wound Rank. Beating the opponent's Assessment roll by 10+ gives **+1k1 on the Focus roll**. — [L5R 4e Mechanics wiki](https://magicalsamurai.wikidot.com/iaijutsu-dueling); [Floating World wiki](https://floating-world-online.fandom.com/wiki/Iaijutsu_Dueling)
- Focus: a **contested Iaijutsu (Focus) / Void roll**. "If one duelist beats the other's roll by 5 or more, that duelist earns the right to make the first strike, and gains a **Free Raise toward his strike roll for every additional increment of 5**." — [L5R 4e Mechanics wiki](https://magicalsamurai.wikidot.com/iaijutsu-dueling)
- Strike: the winner rolls Iaijutsu / Reflexes vs the opponent's normal Armor TN, applying the Free Raises from Focus. — [L5R 4e Mechanics wiki](https://magicalsamurai.wikidot.com/iaijutsu-dueling)
- Design point: the tension sits almost entirely in the pre-strike phases (reading the opponent, then the Focus contest), and the strike itself is one roll. — inferred from the procedure above; see also [philgamer "Let's Study L5R 4e: The Art of the Duel"](https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2010/07/29/lets-study-l5r-4e-the-art-of-the-duel/) (not readable this session)

**Legend of the Five Rings, 5th edition (FFG): strife bidding, Compromised, Unmasking**
- In duels, "initiative is determined with **TN1 Meditation + Staredown (Strife bid)**." — [RPG Crossing L5R 5e Quick Reference](https://www.rpgcrossing.com/showthread.php?p=8934889)
- Staredown procedure as summarized: "at the beginning of each Phase 2: Taking Turns … **each character gains Strife equal to the number of the round**. The characters who are not yet Compromised make **secret bids of a value from 0 to their Focus attribute, which are simultaneously revealed and added to their Strife as well as their Initiative** for the round." — [FFG forum archive "Challenge Focus Strike"](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/304798-challenge-focus-strike-iaijutsu-dueling-rules/); [lasthaiku wikidot Combat](http://lasthaiku.wikidot.com/combat). *Flag:* the snippet may paraphrase core rules or a forum/fan write-up. It is consistent with the actual-play below.
- Actual play: "both players take one Strife for the round and then both bid zero for the Staredown". A character switches to Water Stance and uses the technique "Iaijutsu Cut: Rising Blade" to **draw, ready and strike in one action**. The duel options include duel-specific actions such as **"predict" and "center"**. "If neither duelist is dead at the end of the Strike phase, and if the duel is to the death, the duel becomes a standard skirmish." — [Shiro Daidoji "Rules Runs Part II: A Duel to the Death"](http://shirodaidoji.blogspot.com/2019/02/l5r-rpg-rules-runs-part-ii-duel-to-death.html)
- Air ring use in duels: you can "assess one foe's weakness and learn one disadvantage" of theirs. — [Scribd L5R 5e Quick Reference](https://www.scribd.com/document/582976283/5e-Cheat-Sheet)
- Strife: when strife exceeds Composure the character is **Compromised** and "cannot keep any dice showing strife symbols." **Unmasking** removes all strife: the player roleplays the façade falling, and the GM sets consequences. One Unmask option is a **Challenge of Honor** (demanding a duel). In structured time you can unmask at the start or end of your turn. — [FFG forum archive "Strife and Unmasking"](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/281888-strife-and-unmasking-article-is-up/); [RPG Crossing quick ref](https://www.rpgcrossing.com/showthread.php?p=8934889)

**7th Sea 2nd edition (Duelist Maneuvers, Raises, Styles)**
- Only a Duelist may take the duelist actions (named in the summaries as slash, parry, lunge, bash, riposte, plus feint and beat in other threads). Anyone may use the basic maneuvers. A non-duelist who wants several wounds at once must spend several Raises as one action. — [7thsea2e.com "Raises, Damage, and Maneuvers"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/raises-damage-and-maneuvers); [7thsea2e.com "QA: Duelists & Maneuvers"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/qa-duelists-maneuvers)
- Official text quoted on the forum: "**A Duelist may perform a Maneuver twice in the same Round of an Action Sequence, but there must be an additional Maneuver (or nondueling Action) between the repeated Maneuvers.**" Forum readers say **Slash is the one exemption** (p. 235). Summary: "you can't just slash everyone to death all the time — you need to mix it up." — [7thsea2e.com "Dueling 2E"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/dueling-2e); [7thsea2e.com "Riposte / Parry Maneuvers – Order of Actions"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/riposte-parry-maneuvers-order-actions). *Flag:* the Slash exemption is the forum's reading and may be contested.
- Style bonuses are per-school twists. Examples: **Lakedaimon Agoge**, "Your Lunges require 3 Raises to perform, but they do damage equal to your Weaponry Ranks +2"; **Hallbjorn**, "Feints are replaced with Slams. Slams do 1 immediate damage in addition to the normal Feint effect of setting up your target to take +2 extra Wounds the next time they take Wounds this Round". Several style maneuvers (Aldana Ruse, Leegstra's Crash, Matador's Flourish) are "once per round". — [7thsea2e.com "Duelist styles"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/duelist-styles); [7thsea2e.com "Analysis of Duelist Combat / Styles"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/analysis-duelist-combat-styles)
- The community spent a lot of discussion on maneuver ambiguity and balance (threads titled "Duelists: Problems and Solutions" and "My changes to balance the fighting rules"). — [7thsea2e.com "Duelists: Problems and Solutions"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/duelists-problems-and-solutions); [7thsea2e.com "My changes…"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/my-changes-balance-fighting-rules-updated)

**The Riddle of Steel (Combat Pool bidding)**
- Combat Pool (CP) = **Reflex + Combat Proficiency rank** for melee (Aim + proficiency for ranged). It is both a stat and a pool of dice spent over a round. — [TROS Wiki "Combat Pool"](http://tros.thewestwinds.net/index.php?title=Combat_Pool)
- An exchange is a 1–2 second "exchange of blows". It starts with a **stance declaration (aggressive, defensive, neutral)**, then "a declaration of aggressive or defensive actions **based on simultaneous call**. Both parties grab a red die and a white die and throw one simultaneously." — [EN World "D20 version of Riddle of Steel's simultaneous combat?"](https://www.enworld.org/threads/d20-version-of-riddle-of-steels-simultaneous-combat.269648/)
- The pool must last two exchanges. Example: a CP 12 fighter puts 4 dice on attack, the defender puts 6 on defense, and whoever wins initiative attacks with what remains. "Combat pools refresh after every 2 attack/defend exchanges (except those lost to wounds)." Wounds reduce the pool, so the fight spirals. — [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/15/15097.phtml); [TROS Wiki "Combat"](http://tros.thewestwinds.net/index.php?title=Combat)
- **Spiritual Attributes (SAs)** (Faith, Passion, Conscience, Drive, Destiny, Luck) add dice when the fight concerns them. E.g., "Passion (Lover) 4 and MA 3 … you can add 4 dice to your combat pool 3 times in your fight to save your lover." SA dice "are added to the combat pool and refresh just like the combat pool normally would." Using SAs is also how characters advance. — [RPGnet thread "Help with Spiritual Attributes"](https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/help-with-spiritual-attributes-from-the-riddle-of-steel.9055/); [Project Rho TROS page](https://www.projectrho.com/game/riddleofsteel.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Riddle_of_Steel)

**Burning Wheel (Fight! and Duel of Wits scripting)**
- An exchange has **three volleys**. Players **script 3+ actions ahead**, and at each volley "you stop, look at what each of you scripted, and then roll". Specific action pairings have specific results. — [Lythia/HârnForum thread](https://lythia.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=6821); [RPGnet "Explain Burning Wheel's Duel of Wits"](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/explain-burning-wheels-duel-of-wits-to-me.760950/)
- Duel of Wits (social duel) actions: **Point, Rebuttal, Obfuscate, Feint, Avoid the Topic, Dismiss, Incite**. Point is the main attack. Dismiss adds +2D, and its successes come off the opponent's **Body of Argument**. Starting Body of Argument = **Will exponent + skill successes**. The goal is to reduce the opponent's Body of Argument to zero. — [4pcdn DoW sheet](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1496110160151.pdf); [Scribd "Duel of Wits Mechanics Guide"](https://www.scribd.com/document/700356245/Duel-of-Wits-Draft)
- Scripting is "one of the more contentious elements" and some find the multilayered round annoyingly complex. An RPGnet thread argues that **removing scripting is a bad idea** because the hidden commitment is the point. Fans built card decks and web tools to make scripting usable. — [RPGnet "Axing scripting is a bad idea"](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/burning-wheel-axing-scripting-is-a-bad-idea-lets-talk-about-why.710531/); [A Lazy Sequence: BW action cards](https://brehaut.net/blog/2020/burning_wheel_action_cards/); [GitHub BW Scripter](https://github.com/Daenyth/Burning-Wheel-Scripter)

**Mouse Guard / Torchbearer (conflict scripting)**
- "Each team chooses three actions from Attack, Defend, Feint, Maneuver. **Actions are chosen in sets of three, then revealed one at a time.**" — [Roll20 forum "Mouse Guard combat system"](https://app.roll20.net/forum/post/3779/mouse-guard-combat-system-doable)
- The official game has "three decks of 12 action cards, each of the four actions repeated three times". Each side places cards face-down and flips them in turn. The same four actions resolve **any** conflict type: "a race … a public debate, even a cooking competition". — [Mouse Guard Wiki card list](https://mouseguard.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_Mouse_Guard:_Roleplaying_Game_cards); [Coronas Hide on Mouse Guard conflict](http://blogs.wylfing.net/coronas/2011/09/mouse-guard-setting-and-conflict/); [BW Forums "What beats Attack?"](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/what-beats-attack/12426)

**Exalted 3rd edition (withering and decisive, momentum)**
- **Withering** attacks deal damage to the target's Initiative and **add it to yours**, plus +1 for hitting. **Decisive** attacks spend banked Initiative as damage dice, and afterwards your Initiative **resets to 3**. You cannot make decisive attacks while in **Crash** (0 or less Initiative). — [Ekorren "Exalted 3E: The Basics of Combat"](https://www.patreon.com/ekorren/posts/manuscript-3e-of-28262156); [Exalted 3e wiki Combat 301](http://exalted3e.wikidot.com/ess:combat-301); [attack resolution gist](https://gist.github.com/drumanagh/01ebace965e8b66066d358874a8b3042)
- Crashing someone grants **Initiative Break (+5 Initiative)**. If the crashed character crashes you back ("Crash return"), they reset to base 3, add a new Join Battle roll, and **get an immediate extra attack**. This rules-level comeback mechanic fits a shonen "turn the tables" moment. — [Ekorren Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/ekorren/posts/manuscript-3e-of-28262156); [Combat 301](http://exalted3e.wikidot.com/ess:combat-301)

**Feng Shui 2 (shot clock)**
- Initiative is an **action count**: a Speed check sets your starting shot, and each action costs a number of "shots". — [Alexandrian FS2 cheat sheet](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/43049/roleplaying-games/feng-shui-2-system-cheat-sheet); [Age of Ravens FS2 system](http://ageofravens.blogspot.com/2017/01/blam-blam-epigram-playing-feng-shui-2.html)
- **Fortune**: spend 1 to add +1d6 (positive die) to a check, even after rolling. — [Alexandrian FS2 cheat sheet](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/43049/roleplaying-games/feng-shui-2-system-cheat-sheet)

**Street Fighter: The Storytelling Game (White Wolf, 1994)**
- Maneuvers are written on **Combat Cards**. The lowest-Speed fighter acts first, but "anyone with a higher speed can **interrupt** the move at any time". Combos give a Speed bonus to successive moves. The card system was added "to give the players the sense of excitement on what move would the players picked and who made the best decision." — [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/9/9871.phtml); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter:_The_Storytelling_Game); [SFRPG.com FAQ](https://sfrpg.com/street-fighter-storytelling-game-faq/)

**En Garde! (GDW, 1975)**
- Duels are fought by **secretly recording a sequence of twelve one-action turns** (the better duelist's first sequence is six turns). Actions include Close, Cut, Slash, Lunge, Parry/Riposte, Kick, Block, Rest and Surrender, written as strings like "-X-L-X-". Each turn's pair of actions is **cross-referenced on a results table**. — [Reviews from R'lyeh: En Garde!](https://rlyehreviews.blogspot.com/2025/08/1975-en-garde.html); [Chocolate Hammer](https://www.chocolatehammer.org/?p=6383); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_Garde!_(role-playing_game))

**Honor + Intrigue (Basic Action Games; Barbarians of Lemuria engine)**
- The fencing rules "emphasize using maneuvers and **pressing advantage** over your opponent rather than simply taking turns hitting each other". There are multiple fencing styles. **Fortune Points** can boost a passive defense for a "**Close Call**". *The Duelist's Guide* adds 17 styles and the maneuvers **Goad, Horsemanship, Study Opponent, Forefend**. — [DriveThruRPG product page](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/99286/honor-intrigue); [Bundle of Holding](https://bundleofholding.com/quick/HonorIntrigue); [PDFCoffee Duelist's Guide](https://pdfcoffee.com/honorintrigue-the-duelistx27s-guide-pdf-free.html)

**Shonen-specific duel hacks (fan design)**
- An RPGnet design thread proposes a "duel mechanic made especially for Shonen anime". Characters **power up after losing HP** ("2nd mode"), and flashbacks such as "My friends counted on me" serve as mechanical triggers. — [RPGnet "A duel mechanic (made especially for Shonen anime)"](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/a-duel-mechanic-made-especially-for-shonen-anime.440629/)

### Inferences
- **Hidden simultaneous choice is the most reliable tension generator**, and it scales from light (Mouse Guard's 4 actions revealed in sequence) to heavy (En Garde!'s 12-turn script, BW's volleys). For a Saint Seiya game, a 3-beat script per exchange with 4–5 action types (e.g., Attack / Defend / Feint / Burn Cosmo / Read) seems the sweet spot: it lets a player "see through the enemy's technique the second time" by recognizing patterns. The 7th Sea no-repeat rule, and the Saint Seiya trope "the same technique doesn't work twice on a Saint", can be modeled as a penalty for repeating an action or technique against the same foe.
- **A self-inflicted bid is the best analogue for burning Cosmo.** L5R 5e's Staredown has everyone secretly bid strife for initiative, with the risk of becoming Compromised. Saint Seiya can reuse the structure: secretly bid Cosmo, reveal together, and the higher bid acts first or hits harder, but the bid feeds exhaustion or injury toward a "breaking point". L5R's Unmask, which turns breaking into a dramatic outburst, is a good model for awakening the Seventh Sense.
- **Momentum then cash-out** (Exalted withering then decisive; L5R Focus then Free Raises then one Strike) fits anime duels where the fighters trade blows and then one final technique decides everything. Exalted's crash-return (an immediate extra attack when you reverse a crash) gives a rules-supported comeback beat.
- **A dice pool shared between attack and defense** (Riddle of Steel) builds "reading the opponent" into every exchange, and its Spiritual Attribute bonus, "+X dice when fighting for your Passion", is a direct model for "I fight for Athena / for my brothers".
- Because each duel is 1v1 and long, the procedure must be **fast per beat**. Complexity complaints about BW scripting and 7th Sea maneuvers suggest a reference card per player (as fans built for BW and Mouse Guard).

### Gaps
- Could not read the L5R 5e core duel text. I could not confirm the exact names and effects of the duel-only actions "Center" and "Predict", or whether "Strife = round number" is RAW or a fan write-up. *Recalled, unverified:* in 5e core, Center (Void) is a meditation action that improves your next strike, and Predict (Air) is guessing the opponent's next action for a bonus.
- 7th Sea 2e exact per-maneuver numbers (Slash, Parry, Riposte, Lunge, Feint, Bash, Beat) could not be confirmed. *Recalled, unverified:* Slash deals Wounds equal to Weaponry ranks; Parry prevents Wounds equal to Weaponry and can't be repeated consecutively; Lunge spends all remaining Raises for big damage; Feint sets up extra Wounds.

## 2. "Audience/crowd" participation: mechanics that let non-acting players reward, fuel, or shape the active player's scene

### Takeaway
The strongest precedents give non-acting players a **currency they hand to the active player**, never to themselves: Tenra Bansho Zero's Aiki (audience awards, converted to Kiai), Primetime Adventures' fan mail (an Audience Pool funded by the GM's budget), Fate's passed free invocations, Blades' assist (paid with the helper's own stress) and Devil's Bargains proposed by any player, Kids on Bikes' adversity tokens, and Masks' Team pool. A second family gives non-actors **narrative authority** over the active scene: Fiasco (the others choose the outcome die), Wushu (others' contributed details are fact), and Blades' Devil's Bargains. Both families keep spectators engaged even when their characters are elsewhere.

### Cited Findings

**Tenra Bansho Zero (Aiki, Kiai, Karma)**
- Aiki is awarded when players "play to their Fates, do something particularly cool or dramatic, or otherwise kick the game up to the next level." There are two methods. **Audience**: "each player keeps a side reserve of **three Aiki chits per scene**, and whenever a character does something, any other player can award them Aiki for it (but **no more than one Aiki can be earned per action**)." **Scene Judge**: "the GM picks one player each scene, and they are the sole arbiter of who gets Aiki for that scene" (optional rule for beginners, rotating each scene). — [RPG Writeups: Tenra Bansho Zero (ProfessorProf)](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/); [Scribd TBZ Cheat Sheet](https://www.scribd.com/document/497720410/254991957-Tenra-Bansho-Zero-Cheat-Sheet-PDF)
- Conversion: during **Intermission** between acts, a character spends any number of Aiki to make **Fate rolls (Fate as skill, Empathy as attribute)**, and each success gives 1 Kiai. Aiki can also be spent directly as Kiai, "**negating the usual Karma increase**". Karma is gained when Kiai is spent, and too much Karma is the corruption track. — [RPG Writeups TBZ](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/); [TBZ rulebook PDF](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1479324409010.pdf)
- Kiai spends (as summarized): any amount of Kiai adds that many dice. **3 Kiai** raises a skill rank by 1 for one roll (max 4). After a roll, 3 Kiai each adds a success. **2 Kiai lets you interrupt** another's action and act immediately. Kiai buys extra actions in combat. **"If an ally takes damage, you can spend one Kiai to intercept the damage and take it yourself."** Kiai can also fudge the Emotion Matrix or buy equipment. — [RPG Writeups TBZ](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/); [Mythcreants "TBZ Is Fun and Bizarre"](https://mythcreants.com/blog/tenra-bansho-zero-is-fun-insanity/); [Deeper in the Game "TBZ: Working the Mechanics"](https://bankuei.wordpress.com/2013/12/02/tenra-bansho-zero-working-the-mechanics/)

**Primetime Adventures (Fan Mail and Audience Pool)**
- "When the Producer allocates Budget to a conflict, **an equal number of coins goes into a matching fund called the Audience Pool**. Audience tokens become fan mail when any player wants to signal to another player that they did something awesome." Fan mail can be spent "to **bring your character into scenes** or add cards to the hands" in a conflict. Spent fan mail can flow back into the Producer's Budget, which gives the GM "more dramatic gas". — [RPGnet wiki "How to Run: Primetime Adventures"](https://wiki.rpg.net/index.php/How_to_Run:Primetime_Adventures); [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/13/13243.phtml); ["PTA For Busy People" rules summary](http://frotz.zork.net/~nick/loyhargil/pta/rules.html)

**Fate Core (passing free invocations, teamwork)**
- Succeeding at **Create an Advantage** gives a situation aspect with a free invocation, or two on success with style. "**You can pass your free invocation to another character**… have everyone create an advantage and pass their free invocations onto one person, then that person stacks all of them up at once for a huge bonus." — [Fate SRD "Four Actions"](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions); [Fate SRD "Invoking & Compelling Aspects"](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/invoking-compelling-aspects)
- There are three ways to help: combine the same skill (bonus on one roll), stack free invokes via Create an Advantage, and **invoke aspects on an ally's behalf**. — [Step into RPGs "Fastball Special: Creative teamwork in Fate"](https://stepintorpgs.wordpress.com/2015/07/19/fastball-special-creative-teamwork-in-fate/)

**Blades in the Dark (teamwork, Devil's Bargain)**
- **Assist**: "describe what your character does to help. **Take 1 stress and give them +1d** to their roll. **Only one character may assist a given roll.**" **Protect**: take the harm or consequences for a teammate. **Set Up**: an indirect action, where a success gives whoever follows through **+1 effect level or improved position**. — [Blades SRD (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md); [Roll20 BitD Teamwork](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Teamwork); [bladesinthedark.com Teamwork](https://bladesinthedark.com/teamwork)
- **Devil's Bargain**: "The GM **or any other player** can offer you a bonus die if you accept a Devil's Bargain." It is always a free choice. Suggested table rule: "never ask for it, but suggest something for it… don't make someone else do the work." — [Some Notes on Blades in the Dark (L. Chabant)](https://ludovic.chabant.com/blog/2022/12/27/some-notes-on-blades-in-the-dark/); [Mythcreants on Blades](https://mythcreants.com/blog/blades-in-the-dark-is-innovative-but-difficult-to-run/)
- **Flashbacks**: stress is charged only if the flashback is an unlikely opportunity or complex task. In one playtest campaign "something like two thirds of flashbacks didn't cost Stress". — [Blades G+ archive "flashbacks"](https://bitd.gplusarchive.online/2016/03/12/one-of-the-great-things-about-blades-in-the-dark-is-flashbacks/)

**Masks: A New Generation (Team pool, Comfort or Support)**
- Team points in the shared pool are "normally spent to add to a teammate's roll by helping them". **Act selfishly** lets you take from the pool for your own roll, at the cost of shifting Labels. A playbook ability ("spend 1 burn to supercharge a teammate's efforts… +1 bonus to their roll as if you had spent Team") shows powers used as support. — [Masks Basic Moves (flwlib PDF)](https://www.flwlib.org/DocumentCenter/View/2471/Masks-Basic-Moves); [Masks Wiki Basic Moves](https://masks.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Moves). *Flag:* one summary said "spend 2 to add +2", so the exact exchange rate (likely +1 per Team) needs checking.
- **Comfort or Support**: roll +Mundane. On a hit "they hear you: they **mark potential, clear a condition, or shift Labels if they open up to you**". On 10+ you also **add a Team to the pool** or clear one of your own conditions. "The recipient… must truly open up in response to get any benefit." — [Masks Wiki Basic Moves](https://masks.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Moves); [Stardust & Sigil review](https://theanxiousgamer.wordpress.com/2017/04/05/review-masks-a-new-generation/)

**Marvel Heroic Roleplaying / Cortex (Affiliation, Plot Points, assets)**
- **Affiliation**: each hero has **Solo, Buddy, Team** at d10/d8/d6 in some order. "You don't get the die for what you're best at, but the die that applies to the current circumstances," i.e., alone, with one ally, or with a group. — [Marvel Heroic RP Wiki Affiliations](https://marvelheroicrp.fandom.com/wiki/Affiliations); [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/15/15570.phtml)
- **Plot Points** activate special effects, add a die, or keep an extra die. They are earned partly by accepting bad outcomes, and the GM ("the Watcher") earns Doom Pool dice on players' 1s ("Opportunities"). An effect die can **create an asset** that the *next* hero uses, e.g., a manhole cover another hero throws. — [RPGnet review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/15/15570.phtml); [Beej's blog "What Makes MHR Unique"](https://professorbeej.com/system-shock-what-makes-marvel-heroic-roleplaying-unique/); [d20 Radio "The Doom Pool"](https://www.d20radio.com/main/rules-lawyer-the-doom-pool-reserve/)
- **Cortex Prime help**: "When another PC is in the scene with you, they can lend their help… instead of rolling their own action, and you roll an additional die." Resources and stress from such rolls go **only to the main roller**. You can **step up assets you create to assist other characters**. — [Cannibal Halfling Cortex Prime review](https://cannibalhalflinggaming.com/2020/10/21/cortex-prime-review/); [RPGnet wiki Cortex Prime: Hexen Ward](https://wiki.rpg.net/index.php/Cortex_Prime:_Hexen_Ward)

**Kids on Bikes (adversity tokens)**
- A failed check earns an adversity token. After rolling you can spend any number for +1 each, and "**players can also spend tokens this way to give other players a +1 per token**". In Planned Actions, "every player involved in the scene may contribute their adversity tokens" to the roller. — [Gnome Stew Kids on Bikes review](https://gnomestew.com/take-me-to-the-upside-down-a-review-of-kids-on-bikes/); [Gamers' Plane KoB 2E rules summary](https://gamersplane.com/forums/thread/30236/)

**Mutants & Masterminds 3e (Aid, Team checks)**
- **Aid** (standard action): if you're in position to attack the opponent, make an attack check vs DC 10. Success gives an ally **+2 circumstance to attack *or* Defense vs that opponent**, and three or more degrees of success give **+5**. The **Teamwork** advantage gives +5 when supporting team checks, Aid and Team Attacks. — [d20HeroSRD Advantages](https://www.d20herosrd.com/5-advantages/); [M&M GM's Resource "Teamwork"](https://d3mm2.blogspot.com/2016/03/teamwork.html)

**Savage Worlds**
- The **Common Bond** Edge lets a hero freely give Bennies to others. — [Savage Worlds Rules Summary (System sans Setting)](https://samhaine.wordpress.com/2020/04/06/savage-worlds-rules-summary/); [Roll20 wiki Savage Worlds](https://wiki.roll20.net/Savage_Worlds)

**Narrative-authority mechanics**
- **Fiasco**: on your scene you choose **Establish** (you set up the scene, and "the resolution… is determined by the other players, who will choose a light die (good) or a dark die (bad)") or **Resolve** (the others set up the scene, and you choose the outcome). — [Gnome Stew "Fiasco: Rules, Dice…"](https://gnomestew.com/fiasco-rules-dice-friends-trouble/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiasco_(role-playing_game))
- **Wushu**: the "Principle of Narrative Truth: everything the players describe happens exactly as they describe it"; each distinct detail = 1d6. Another player's description "is fact, don't touch it… you may use other's descriptions as a building block", and any player can **veto** an out-of-tone detail. — [Wushu "Playing the Game" (Daniel Bayn)](https://danielbayn.com/wushu/playing-the-game/index.shtml); [Philosophy of Games Wushu overview](http://philosophyofgames.blogspot.com/2008/12/wushu-overviewreview.html)
- **HeroQuest 2**: abilities, including **relationships**, can augment the main ability, and **other characters can grant augments** ("in asymmetrical exchanges during contests, you can grant augments to an ally"). — [Sarah Newton "Musings on HeroQuest 2nd edition (part 2)"](https://sarahnewtonwriter.com/2011/12/13/asymmetric-gaming-musings-on-heroquest-2nd-edition-part-2/); [HeroQuest Glorantha Rules Summary](https://lyssa-rpg-docs.neocities.org/hq-glorantha/rules-summary)

### Inferences
- **TBZ's Aiki is the closest mechanical match to "friends sending Cosmo".** Absent players hold a per-scene budget (three chits, at most one award per action) and hand it to the fighter for great moments. The fighter later turns it into power (Kiai), and spending power has a corruption or overload cost (Karma). A Saint Seiya version could let non-dueling players give **Cosmo tokens** during the duel itself, not only at intermission. The one-per-action and three-per-scene caps prevent dogpiling.
- **PTA's link from GM budget to the Audience Pool** suits "Athena's presence". The GM (or an Athena/Goddess track) spending on threats refills the pool the audience gives from. Harder fights generate more support currency, which mirrors "the harder the Gold Saint, the more Athena and friends pour Cosmo in".
- **Paid help beats free help.** Blades (assist costs 1 stress, one assister per roll) and TBZ (intercepting damage costs Kiai) make support a sacrifice. That fits Saint Seiya (e.g., Hyoga or Shun burning their own life to help), and it keeps support from being a passive free buff.
- **Masks' Comfort or Support requires reciprocity**: the downed hero benefits only if they open up. That is the "get up, Seiya!" beat, and it gives the fallen player an active choice too.
- **Fiasco and Wushu show spectators can hold narrative dice**. Example: the watching players choose whether the fighter's next exchange ends in a "light" or "dark" complication. This gives them a meaningful vote without a character present.
- **Marvel Heroic's Affiliation dice** reward acting alone in a system where teams exist. A Saint whose best die is Solo shines in 1v1 duels, and others may be better as Buddy or Team, which argues for varied builds.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether TBZ allows Aiki awards to players whose characters are **not in the scene** (the Audience method implies any player at the table can award, but this wasn't confirmed).
- Savage Worlds' Support roll values (*recalled, unverified:* success +1, raise +2, max +4 from multiple supporters) were not confirmed.
- Not researched this session: 13th Age backgrounds, Dread (Jenga), Honey Heist, Brindlewood Bay, Polaris, Microscope. *Recalled, unverified:* Polaris assigns non-active players fixed adversarial or supporting roles (the "Mistaken" and the "Moons") so every player has a job in every scene, which may be a strong model for spectators, but I could not verify it this session.
- Blades "Lead a Group Action" wasn't confirmed. *Recalled, unverified:* each participant rolls, the best result counts, and the leader takes 1 stress per failed roll.

## 3. Split-party mechanics: spotlight when the group is separated, and remote support actions ("support from afar")

### Takeaway
The main split-party tool is **scene-framing technique**, not rules: cut between simultaneous scenes mid-scene on cliffhangers, and line up crossovers where one scene's outcome affects another (the Alexandrian). Other tools: hand absent players NPCs (Gnome Stew: works well in action play, poorly in mysteries), and give idle players jobs such as inventing consequences. Explicit "support from afar" rules are rare. The closest are currency transfers that the rules don't tie to physical presence (Fate passed invokes, TBZ Aiki from the audience, PTA fan mail that can **bring a character into a scene**, Kids on Bikes tokens, Savage Worlds Common Bond) and relationship-based augments (HeroQuest). Cortex help and M&M Aid explicitly **require presence or position**, which shows that designers deliberately choose whether help is local or remote.

### Cited Findings
- **The Alexandrian (split parties)**: "Running simultaneous scenes – which is the end result of splitting the party – is basically **Easy Mode for effective pacing**." Cut **in the middle of scenes** rather than playing full scenes in sequence. This also makes it easier "to smoothly cut past empty time and refocus on the next interesting choice." Advanced technique: look for **crossovers**, "moments when elements or outcomes from one scene have an impact on another scene", and line them up. — [Alexandrian "Random GM Tip: Splitting the Party"](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/47259/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-splitting-the-party); [Alexandrian "The Art of Pacing – Part 5"](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/33791/roleplaying-games/the-art-of-pacing-part-5-advanced-techniques); [Alexandrian "Cutting on the Cliffhanger"](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/48619/roleplaying-games/ptolus-running-the-campaign-cutting-on-the-cliffhanger)
- **Gnome Stew**: handing NPCs to unengaged players "doesn't seem viable in mystery games". In one example, "a PC was kidnapped, and the DM brought in an NPC for him to play. That worked quite well – the player had plenty to do and was still working with the group." With a split party, NPC relationships "become even more important", and NPCs tied to one sub-party can show up for the other. — [Gnome Stew "Splitting the Party"](https://gnomestew.com/splitting-the-party/); [Gnome Stew "Splitting the Party: 5 Approaches"](https://gnomestew.com/splitting-the-party-5-approaches/); [Gnome Stew "Starting With A Split Party"](https://gnomestew.com/staring-with-a-split-party/)
- "As a GM, it is important to **keep the spotlight moving around the table**." — [Gnome Stew "The Teleporting Player"](https://gnomestew.com/the-teleporting-player/)
- **Give idle players a job**: when one player has the spotlight, the others can help come up with interesting consequences for that player. — [Jacke substack "When one player is in the spotlight, give everyone else a job"](https://jacke.substack.com/p/when-one-player-is-in-the-spotlight)
- **Local vs remote help in the rules text**: Cortex Prime help requires that the helper be "**in the scene with you**" ([Cannibal Halfling review](https://cannibalhalflinggaming.com/2020/10/21/cortex-prime-review/)). M&M Aid requires that "you are **in position to attack** an opponent" ([d20HeroSRD](https://www.d20herosrd.com/5-advantages/)). Kids on Bikes Planned Actions let "every player **involved in the scene**" contribute tokens ([Gamers' Plane KoB 2E summary](https://gamersplane.com/forums/thread/30236/)).
- **Currency that can cross scene boundaries**: PTA fan mail can be spent "to **bring your character into scenes**" ([RPGnet wiki PTA](https://wiki.rpg.net/index.php/How_to_Run:Primetime_Adventures)). TBZ Audience method: "any other player can award them Aiki" ([RPG Writeups TBZ](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/)). Fate: "you can pass your free invocation to another character" ([Fate SRD Four Actions](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions)).
- **Split party as a mechanical state**: Marvel Heroic's Affiliation dice change with how many allies are present (Solo, Buddy, Team). — [Marvel Heroic RP Wiki Affiliations](https://marvelheroicrp.fandom.com/wiki/Affiliations)
- **Fiasco** is built entirely around rotating scenes: every player gets a scene per round and chooses Establish or Resolve, and the non-featured players still decide the outcome die or frame the scene. — [Gnome Stew Fiasco](https://gnomestew.com/fiasco-rules-dice-friends-trouble/)

### Inferences
- A structure like the 12 Houses (heroes at different Houses in parallel) is exactly the Alexandrian's "simultaneous scenes" case. A duel system with **discrete beats** (e.g., 3-volley exchanges) gives natural cut points: cut after each exchange, and a crossover (one hero wins a House, or a hero collapses) can trigger support in another duel.
- Saint Seiya treats Cosmo as crossing space, so **remote support should be an explicit rule exception**, unlike Cortex or M&M's presence requirement. Two candidate models:
  1. **Currency transfer** (Fate invokes, TBZ Aiki, Kids on Bikes tokens) with no presence requirement but a sender cost.
  2. **Relationship-gated augments** (HeroQuest relationships, and Double Cross Lois in section 4): you can only send Cosmo to someone you have a bond with.
- A PTA-style clause, "spend support currency to **enter** a scene", could model a friend arriving at the last second, a classic Saint Seiya beat, as a paid player-driven intrusion instead of GM fiat.
- Player-controlled NPCs (Gnome Stew): absent players could run the opposing fighter's minor tactics, or the "audience NPCs" (other Saints and spectators) during a duel. The Gnome Stew caveat is about mysteries, not action, so it seems workable for duels.

### Gaps
- I found **no system with an explicitly named "support from afar / across distance" action**. Everything above is either a presence-agnostic currency or a GM technique. This is a real gap in the prior art and a design opportunity.
- Could not read Angry GM content on split parties. Could not find a quantified guideline for how long to stay in each sub-scene.
- Ars Magica troupe play and Polaris-style role distribution were not verified.

## 4. Spectators, mentors, and allies' presence buffing a fighter (rules for "being watched / being fought for")

### Takeaway
Buffs from presence or relationship appear in four forms: (1) **transfer of momentum** (Exalted's Distract gambit hands your lost Initiative to an ally's next decisive attack; Feng Shui 2's Boost spends your shots on an ally's attack or defense), (2) **damage interception** (TBZ's 1 Kiai intercept, Blades' Protect, Exalted's Defend Other), (3) **motivation dice** keyed to what or who you fight for (Riddle of Steel Passions/Drives, HeroQuest relationships), and (4) **bond-burning comeback effects** (Double Cross Lois/Titus and the genre-standard "Resurrection" power; *My Friends Are My Strength*'s bonds and Plot Armor; Masks' Comfort or Support). The last two families are closest to Saint Seiya's cheering and Athena beats.

### Cited Findings
- **Exalted 3e Distract gambit** (difficulty 3–5): lead or feint the target "into the path of an ally's decisive attack. The attacker declares an ally (not in Initiative Crash) as the beneficiary… that ally **gains the Initiative the character loses** as a result". It "must be used to attack the gambit's target on the ally's next turn, or it is lost". One distraction bonus at a time. A forum tactic: a character with weak decisive attacks feeds Initiative to a heavy hitter. — [Camarades Soleil wiki "Distract (Gambit)"](https://camarades-soleil.fandom.com/wiki/Distract_(Gambit)); [Onyx Path forum "Questions about 3e tactics"](https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/exalted/938523-questions-about-3e-tactics)
- **Exalted 3e Defend Other** exists as a combat action, and a forum user rates "Defend Other and gang-up on a single target" as "incredibly good". — [Onyx Path forum "Questions about 3e tactics"](https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/exalted/938523-questions-about-3e-tactics); [Exalted 3e combat cheat sheet](https://bragrman.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/exalted-3e-combat-cheat-sheet-2.pdf)
- **Feng Shui 2 Boost**: a **3-shot action** to help another character, granting "+1 to the recipient's next attack, or +3 to the recipient's Defense against the next attack (and all others in the same shot)". — [Alexandrian Feng Shui tag page](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/tag/feng-shui)
- **TBZ**: "If an ally takes damage, you can spend one Kiai to intercept the damage and take it yourself." — [RPG Writeups TBZ](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/)
- **Blades Protect**: "You cover for a teammate, suffering any harm or consequences… It hurts, cost stress." — [Roll20 BitD Teamwork](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Teamwork)
- **Riddle of Steel**: Passion dice apply when fighting *for* the object of passion (e.g., to save your lover). — [RPGnet SA thread](https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/help-with-spiritual-attributes-from-the-riddle-of-steel.9055/)
- **Double Cross 3rd (Japanese RPG)**: **Lois** are bonds with people "who help to keep you connected to the real world"; each Lois held at session end reduces Encroachment (corruption) by 1D10. A Lois can become a **Titus** (a relationship spent or abused), and "the Overed may **discard that Titus for a one time bonus**, using up everything that is left in that relationship." Every Overed starts with a **Resurrection** power, described as "the genre-mandatory '**get up after you were severely beaten down**' effect". — [Faustus Notes "Double Cross 3: Titus and Lois"](https://faustusnotes.com/2010/07/25/japanese-rpg-double-cross-3-titus-and-lois/); [RPGnet Double Cross review](https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/16/16110.phtml); [Deeper in the Game "Double Cross RPG"](https://bankuei.wordpress.com/2014/02/12/double-cross-rpg/)
- **My Friends Are My Strength** (PbtA shonen RPG): bonds "serve as the foundation of their strength", and plot armor is "woven into the mechanics, allowing characters to survive otherwise deadly encounters through the strength of their bonds". "Every Protagonist gets **3 uses of Plot Armor to turn any roll into a complete success**." — [All Ages of Geek on MFAMS](https://allagesofgeek.com/my-friends-are-my-strength-ttrpg/); [MFAMS itch.io page](https://peizur.itch.io/mfams)
- **Shonen fan design**: an "affinity system which **boosts allies when someone with good affinity is defeated** in battle", plus transformations and protection between allies. (Summary drew on an itch.io "Shonen RPG" and the RPGnet thread. Attribution between them is uncertain.) — [Shonen RPG (itch.io)](https://pau-barto.itch.io/shonen-rpg); [RPGnet shonen duel mechanic thread](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/a-duel-mechanic-made-especially-for-shonen-anime.440629/)
- **Masks Comfort or Support** clears conditions or marks potential only if the recipient opens up (see section 2). — [Masks Wiki Basic Moves](https://masks.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Moves)
- **Fate** lets you create an advantage and pass the free invocation to an ally. The advantage is a narrative fact, so it doesn't need to be a physical action next to the ally. — [Fate SRD Four Actions](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions)
- **HeroQuest 2 relationships** are abilities that can augment the main ability in a contest. — [Sarah Newton on HQ2](https://sarahnewtonwriter.com/2011/12/13/asymmetric-gaming-musings-on-heroquest-2nd-edition-part-2/)

### Inferences
- **The "fallen hero gets up" beat** has strong precedent in Double Cross (a Resurrection power plus burning a bond for a one-time bonus), MFAMS (limited Plot Armor uses plus bonds), and Masks (clearing a condition requires the downed hero to open up). A Saint Seiya rule could be: when a Saint is downed, allies (present or not) can spend support currency or bond ranks, and the downed player must answer with a declaration (what they fight for), which ties into Riddle of Steel-style motivation dice. Double Cross's Lois-to-Titus shows how to make it **costly and finite**: a bond used this way is changed or used up.
- **Athena's presence** fits as a **scene-level modifier** rather than a character action. Options: (a) Marvel Heroic-style situational dice that depend on who is present, (b) a PTA-style pool the GM fills, or (c) a TBZ-style Karma or "Encroachment" relief. Double Cross's Lois reducing corruption is a model where closeness to what you protect keeps you from burning out.
- **Momentum transfer** (Exalted Distract) can model "I'll hold him, you strike!" among the few duels that aren't 1v1. The rule that transferred Initiative must be used next turn or is lost prevents stockpiling.
- **Interception** (TBZ, Blades Protect) models Shun's chain defending or Ikki intervening. Paying for it with a cost the protector suffers is the norm across systems.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Double Cross's exact Titus spending options (*recalled, unverified:* they include large dice bonuses, rerolls, and **recovering from Incapacitated**). This is directly relevant to "get up, Seiya!" and should be checked in the Double Cross English core (Japanime Games).
- Exalted 3e **social influence** (Intimacies, "inspire" actions that could buff allies emotionally) was not researched.
- Could not confirm Super Robot Wars-style adjacency bonuses from a reliable tabletop source. The mention was a search-summary aside about a video game.

## 5. Common pitfalls GMs report (downtime, spotlight hogging, passive-feeling support)

### Takeaway
Reported pitfalls: (1) **Idle time in long one-on-one scenes and in turn-based combat.** Remedies: faster resolution procedures and jobs for non-duelists (seconds, terrain, consequences). (2) **Heavy scripting and maneuver complexity** (BW, 7th Sea 2e). Remedies: reference cards and tools. (3) **Dominant team strategies** (Exalted Defend Other and gang-ups). (4) **Social friction in peer-reward systems** (players asking for Devil's Bargains or awards instead of offering them). (5) **Player-controlled NPCs failing in certain genres** (mysteries). Support that is free, automatic, or unlimited tends to feel passive. Precedents that cap and charge for support (Blades' one assister and 1 stress; TBZ's one Aiki per action and three per scene) address this.

### Cited Findings
- **Duels and table boredom**: "A duel doesn't have to be a time for the other players to quietly behave while one player takes the spotlight." Remedies include giving others parallel tasks (lockpicking, fighting guards), using the historical role of **seconds** (witnesses who assist and set rules, time and place), terrain and hazards, and faster resolution (e.g., *Jadepunk* turns duels into a **Fate Contest**, "quick and deadly, and avoids boredom for the other players"). — [Tribality "Duels that are Fun for Everyone at the Table"](https://www.tribality.com/2019/02/01/duels-that-are-fun-for-everyone-at-the-table/); [D&D Beyond forum "How to run a duel?"](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeons-dragons-discussion/rules-game-mechanics/187371-how-to-run-a-duel)
- **Turn wait time**: one search summary said each combat turn "can take 5–7 minutes and with a party of 4–5 people there can be 25 minutes between turns." *Flag:* I could not tie this to one specific source among [Thalassios "RPG design thoughts"](https://thalassios.substack.com/p/rpg-design-thoughts-intro-to-anti) and [Jacke substack](https://jacke.substack.com/p/when-one-player-is-in-the-spotlight). Treat it as an anecdotal estimate.
- **Give idle players jobs** during another's spotlight, e.g., proposing consequences. — [Jacke substack](https://jacke.substack.com/p/when-one-player-is-in-the-spotlight)
- **Scripting complexity**: BW scripting is "one of the more contentious elements", and some find it "annoyingly complex". Fans built cards and apps. The counter-argument is that removing scripting removes the tension. — [Lythia forum](https://lythia.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=6821); [RPGnet "Axing scripting is a bad idea"](https://forum.rpg.net/threads/burning-wheel-axing-scripting-is-a-bad-idea-lets-talk-about-why.710531/); [BW action cards](https://brehaut.net/blog/2020/burning_wheel_action_cards/)
- **7th Sea 2e duel rules produced repeated rules questions** about Raises vs Maneuvers and order of actions, plus fan rebalances. — [7thsea2e "More Dueling Maneuver Questions"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/more-dueling-maneuver-questions); [7thsea2e "Duelists: Problems and Solutions"](https://www.7thsea2e.com/port/forum/duelists-problems-and-solutions)
- **Dominant team tactic**: "Defend Other and gang-up on a single target until they're out of the fight are both incredibly good" (Exalted 3e). — [Onyx Path forum](https://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/main-category/exalted/938523-questions-about-3e-tactics)
- **Peer-offered bonuses create social load**. Suggested Blades table rule: "never ask for [a Devil's Bargain], but suggest something… don't make someone else do the work." — [L. Chabant "Some Notes on Blades in the Dark"](https://ludovic.chabant.com/blog/2022/12/27/some-notes-on-blades-in-the-dark/)
- **Blades caps help**: "Only one character may assist a given roll", and assisting costs 1 stress. — [Roll20 BitD Teamwork](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Teamwork)
- **TBZ caps awards**: at most 1 Aiki per action, three chits per player per scene (Audience method). A Scene Judge variant exists for beginners. — [RPG Writeups TBZ](https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/professorprof/tenra-bansho-zero/)
- **NPC hand-off limits**: player play of NPCs "doesn't seem viable in mystery games", though it worked for a kidnapped-PC situation in action play. — [Gnome Stew "Splitting the Party"](https://gnomestew.com/splitting-the-party/); [Gnome Stew "5 Approaches"](https://gnomestew.com/splitting-the-party-5-approaches/)
- **Masks gates Comfort or Support on the recipient opening up**, which stops support from being mechanical spam. — [Masks Wiki Basic Moves](https://masks.fandom.com/wiki/Basic_Moves)

### Inferences
- In Saint Seiya, most fights are 1v1, so the default risk is **N−1 players idle for most of a session**. The precedents suggest layering three things:
  1. A **fast, beat-based duel procedure** with discrete cut points (Mouse Guard's 3 revealed actions, L5R's stage structure), so the GM can cut between parallel duels (Alexandrian).
  2. **A per-scene support budget** for every non-dueling player, capped per action (TBZ) and costly to the giver (Blades), so each gift is a real decision rather than a reflex.
  3. **Narrative authority for spectators**: choosing complication or outcome dice (Fiasco), proposing Devil's Bargains (Blades), or voicing the opponent's minor tactics and taunts (Gnome Stew's NPC hand-off).
- Support feels passive when it is (a) unlimited, (b) free, (c) invisible to the recipient's choices, or (d) disconnected from the helper's own character arc. Masks' reciprocity requirement and Double Cross's bond transformation address (c) and (d), and Blades and TBZ caps address (a) and (b). The Saint Seiya support action should probably also advance the *sender* (e.g., mark progress on the bond or on their own Cosmo).
- Avoid a dominant strategy where everyone always funnels everything to one fighter (the Exalted gang-up lesson). Caps, one assister per roll, and "use it next turn or lose it" (Exalted Distract) are proven dampers.

### Gaps
- Could not access Reddit or Angry GM directly, so I found no systematic survey of GM complaints about support roles. The claims above come from scattered blog and forum posts.
- No data on how TBZ's Audience method works at real tables (e.g., whether players forget to award Aiki). Black Armada's "Reward rules" article might cover this but was blocked.
- The "25 minutes between turns" figure is anecdotal and weakly attributed.
